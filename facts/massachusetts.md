# Massachusetts — Fact Sheet (facts only, no handbook prose)

Source: Massachusetts Driver's Manual, Revised April 2026 (English PDF, mass.gov). Each line = one fact, tagged with manual chapter/section. Facts only; wording below is compressed notation, not handbook sentences.

## Chapter 1 — Obtaining Your License

- [Ch.1, Who Needs a License] MA resident must hold a valid MA license to drive on a public way.
- [Ch.1, Who Needs a License] New MA resident must get a MA license; no grace period.
- [Ch.1, Age Requirement] No one under 16 may drive in MA, even with an out-of-state license/permit.
- [Ch.1, Age Requirement] Permit application minimum age: 16 (Class D or M).
- [Ch.1, Age Requirement] Earliest license: age 16½ + permit held in good standing 6 consecutive months + RMV-approved driver education completed.
- [Ch.1, Age Requirement] CDL (Class A/B/C) application minimum age: 18.
- [Ch.1, Parent/Guardian Consent] Under-18 applicant needs written consent of parent, legal guardian, social worker, or boarding-school headmaster.
- [Ch.1, Vision Screening] Full license vision standard: at least 20/40 corrected in better eye + at least 120 degrees horizontal peripheral vision (both eyes).
- [Ch.1, Vision Screening] Corrected vision 20/50–20/70 in better eye may qualify for daylight-only license.
- [Ch.1, License Classes] Class A: combination vehicles, GCWR 26,001+ lbs, towed unit over 10,000 lbs GVWR; includes B, C, D privileges with endorsements.
- [Ch.1, License Classes] Class B: single vehicle GVWR 26,001+ lbs, or towing up to 10,000 lbs GVWR; includes C and D.
- [Ch.1, License Classes] Class C: vehicle designed for 16+ passengers incl. driver, or placarded hazmat vehicle; includes D.
- [Ch.1, License Classes] Class D: passenger vehicles (cars, SUVs, family vans, small trucks) not in A/B/C/M.
- [Ch.1, License Classes] Class M: motorcycles.
- [Ch.1, License Length] License valid max 5 years; typically expires on birthday; renewal allowed up to 1 year before expiration.
- [Ch.1, License Length] Under-21 cards are vertical and marked "Under 21"; card stays valid past 21st birthday until expiration.
- [Ch.1, Restrictions] Restriction code B = corrective lenses; G = daylight only; T = ignition interlock; I = Junior Operator / other.
- [Ch.1, REAL ID] REAL ID (star, top right) or other federally accepted ID needed for domestic flights / certain federal facilities; Standard card is not valid for those federal purposes; driving privileges are the same.
- [Ch.1, JOL — Requirements] JOL applicant (16½–18): permit ≥6 consecutive months; clean record for 6 consecutive months before road test; driver ed = 30 hrs classroom + 12 hrs behind-the-wheel + 6 hrs observing other students; 40 hrs supervised driving (30 hrs if driver skills development program completed); parent/guardian 2 hrs curriculum participation.
- [Ch.1, JOL — Restrictions] Passenger restriction, first 6 months of JOL: no passenger under 18 (except self or immediate family) unless accompanied by licensed driver age 21+ with ≥1 year experience seated beside the junior operator.
- [Ch.1, JOL — Restrictions] Night restriction: no driving 12:30 a.m.–5:00 a.m. unless accompanied by a parent or legal guardian; violation can be charged as driving unlicensed (criminal).
- [Ch.1, JOL — Restrictions] Passenger/night restriction violation penalties: 60 days suspension (1st), 180 days (2nd), 1 year (later); 2nd+ also requires Driver Attitudinal Retraining course.
- [Ch.1, JOL — Restrictions] JOL holder may not drive a vehicle requiring a CDL.
- [Ch.1, JOL — Restrictions] JOL speeding: 90 days suspension (1st offense), 1 year (later). JOL drag racing: 1 year (1st), 3 years (later).
- [Ch.1, JOL — Restrictions] Drivers under 18: no mobile electronic device use for any reason while driving; only exception = reporting an emergency.
- [Ch.1, Getting a Learner's Permit] Permit exam fee: $30. Permit valid up to 2 years.
- [Ch.1, Learner's Permit Exam Procedures] Exam: 25 multiple-choice questions; pass = 18 correct; time limit 25 minutes (about 1 minute per question).
- [Ch.1, Learner's Permit Exam Procedures] Using the manual, reference material, or an electronic device during the exam = cheating; being found responsible = no license/permit of any type for 60 days.
- [Ch.1, Foreign Language Tests] Class D and M permit exams offered in many languages incl. Chinese (simplified) and Chinese (traditional); Class A/B/C exams English only.
- [Ch.1, Driving With Your Permit] Class D permit driving: supervisor beside you must be licensed, age 21+, ≥1 year driving experience.
- [Ch.1, Driving With Your Permit] Permit holder under 18: no driving 12:00 a.m.–5:00 a.m. unless accompanied by parent/legal guardian who is a licensed operator with ≥1 year experience.
- [Ch.1, Driving With Your Permit] Class M permit: no passenger; daylight hours only (sunrise–sunset); DOT helmet required.
- [Ch.1, Taking the Road Test] Class D: max 6 road test attempts in 12 months. Failing / no-show: wait at least 14 days for next test. Free cancel/reschedule with more than 72 hours notice.
- [Ch.1, Road Test Sponsor] Class D road test sponsor: licensed, age 21+, ≥1 year experience; foreign license holders cannot sponsor; one operator may sponsor max 3 applicants per year without RMV approval.
- [Ch.1, License/ID Fees] Road test fee $35; Class D or M license issue/renewal $50; duplicate/amended license $25.

## Chapter 2 — Safety First

- [Ch.2, Passenger Vehicle Safety] Vehicle inspection (safety + emissions) required every year.
- [Ch.2, Passenger Vehicle Safety] Window tint: side/rear windows may be darkened up to 35 percent; tinting the windshield is illegal.
- [Ch.2, Safety/Seat Belt Law] Every person in a passenger vehicle (incl. vans/small trucks under 18,000 lbs) must wear a safety belt or be in a child restraint.
- [Ch.2, Safety/Seat Belt Law] Belt fine: $25 for unbelted driver; $25 for unbelted passenger age 16+; driver fined $25 per unbelted passenger age 12–15.
- [Ch.2, Safety/Seat Belt Law] Belt fine can only be issued when stopped for a traffic violation (secondary enforcement).
- [Ch.2, Child Passenger Restraints] Child restraint required until child is at least 8 years old OR at least 57 inches tall; after that, safety belt required; violation fine $25.
- [Ch.2, Child Passenger Restraints] Never place a rear-facing child seat in the front seat of a vehicle with a passenger air bag; back seat (middle best) is safest.
- [Ch.2, Air-Bag Safety] Air bags supplement belts, do not replace them; front air bags protect in head-on crashes only.
- [Ch.2, Inside the Vehicle] Headphones/headset while driving illegal; driver 18+ may use one earplug for a cell phone.
- [Ch.2, Inside the Vehicle] Vehicle TV must be behind the front seat, not visible to driver. Children under 12 may not ride in pickup truck beds (very limited exceptions, never above 5 mph).
- [Ch.2, Hands-Free Law] Hand-held mobile electronic device use while driving prohibited (Ch. 90 §13B; Acts of 2019 Ch. 122; in force 2020).
- [Ch.2, Hands-Free Law] Drivers 18+: hands-free mode only; device may not be held/supported; touch allowed only to activate hands-free mode; device must be mounted on windshield, dashboard, or center console.
- [Ch.2, Hands-Free Law] Hand-held use allowed only when vehicle is stationary and not in a public travel lane; NOT allowed at red lights or stop signs.
- [Ch.2, Hands-Free Law] No writing/sending/reading texts, emails, or internet use while driving; mounted GPS/navigation use allowed.
- [Ch.2, Hands-Free Law] Drivers under 18: no mobile electronic device use at all; only exception = reporting an emergency.
- [Ch.2, Hands-Free Law] Penalties: 1st offense $100; 2nd $250 + distracted driving education program; 3rd+ $500 + insurance surcharge + program.
- [Ch.2, Safe Distances] Following distance: 3-second rule minimum in good conditions; at least 4 seconds behind a motorcycle; at least 10 seconds behind heavy equipment.
- [Ch.2, Safe Distances] Tailgating illegal; fine can be as high as $100. If tailgated: change lanes or pull aside to let the tailgater pass.
- [Ch.2, Braking and Stopping] Reaction time ≈ 3/4 second. Total stopping distance ≈ 215 ft at 50 mph; ≈ 292 ft at 60 mph; ≈ 104 ft at 30 mph (dry, level pavement).
- [Ch.2, Braking and Stopping] Large trucks may need twice the stopping distance of passenger vehicles. Never pump antilock (ABS) brakes.
- [Ch.2, Horn/Headlights/Signals] Headlights + taillights required: ½ hour after sunset to ½ hour before sunrise; when visibility under 500 feet; whenever wipers are in use (daytime running lights not sufficient); in tunnels.
- [Ch.2, Night Driving] High beams: usable view ≈ 350 ft ahead; low beams ≈ 100 ft. Dim high beams within 500 ft of oncoming vehicle and within 200 ft behind a vehicle ahead.
- [Ch.2, Night Driving] Blinded by oncoming lights: look to lower right side of your lane.
- [Ch.2, Rain/Fog] Roads most slippery when rain first mixes with dirt/oil. Hydroplaning response: ease off gas slowly; never brake hard or turn suddenly. Fog: use low beams.
- [Ch.2, Winter Driving] Bridges and overpasses freeze before other road surfaces. Remove ALL snow/ice from vehicle (roof, hood, trunk, windows, lights) before driving; failure can be charged as negligent operation. Keep gas tank at least half full in winter.
- [Ch.2, Vehicle Condition] Tire tread minimum 2/32 inch; penny test — if top of Lincoln's head visible in groove, tire is worn out. License plate number must be visible from 60 feet at night.
- [Ch.2, Vulnerable Road Users Law] Vulnerable users include pedestrians, roadside workers, bicyclists, horse riders/carriages, wheelchair and personal mobility device users, motorized bicycles/scooters, non-motorized scooters, skateboards, roller skates.
- [Ch.2, Vulnerable Road Users Law] Passing a vulnerable road user: leave at least 4 feet; may use part/all of adjacent lane and may cross center line if safe; must obey speed limit while passing.
- [Ch.2, Pedestrians] White Cane Law: stop completely for a blind pedestrian crossing (white cane or guide dog); remain stopped until safely across; do not honk or wave them on; never pass a vehicle stopped for them.
- [Ch.2, Bicycles and Mopeds] Bicyclists/moped riders have same road rights and must obey same traffic laws; exceptions = limited-access/express highways posted no bicycles.
- [Ch.2, Motorcycles] Motorcycle entitled to full lane width; never share/move into a motorcycle's lane space; lane weaving by motorcyclists is illegal. Leave 4-second following distance behind motorcycles.
- [Ch.2, Mopeds] Moped: max speed capability 30 mph, engine ≤50 cc, automatic transmission; operator needs any-class license or permit; moped speed limit in use 25 mph; DOT helmet for driver and passenger; fine up to $100.
- [Ch.2, Motorized Scooters] Scooter max 20 mph; no passengers; DOT helmet; stay right; no driving between sunset and sunrise; not registered by RMV.
- [Ch.2, Limited Use / Low Speed Vehicles] Limited use vehicle: over 30 up to 40 mph; barred from roads with speed limit over 40 mph. Low speed vehicle: 20–25 mph, under 3,000 lbs; barred from roads with speed limit over 30 mph.

## Chapter 3 — Keeping Your License

- [Ch.3, Civil Infractions] Civil citation: pay fine or request hearing within 20 days; hearing filing fee $25 (refunded if found not responsible for all violations); no response in 20 days = found responsible + late fee; unpaid = license suspension.
- [Ch.3, Speeding Violations] Minimum speeding fine $100 + $5 public safety surcharge; over the first 10 mph over limit: +$10 per mph; all speeding fines include $50 Head Injury Treatment Services Trust Fund surcharge. Speeding fine doubled in posted work zone.
- [Ch.3, Surchargeable Events] Surchargeable events = motor vehicle violations + at-fault crashes; most out-of-state convictions count as if in MA.
- [Ch.3, Surchargeable Events] 3 speeding violations in 12 months = automatic 30-day suspension.
- [Ch.3, Surchargeable Events] 3 surchargeable events in 2 years = Driver Retraining course required; complete within 90 days or license suspended until completed. Retraining course = 8 hours (two 4-hour sessions).
- [Ch.3, Surchargeable Events] 7 surchargeable events in 3 years = automatic 60-day suspension.
- [Ch.3, At-Fault Crashes] At fault = more than 50% at fault per Standards of Fault + insurer paid over $1,000 (collision/property/bodily injury thresholds as manual states).
- [Ch.3, Suspensions — 18+] Habitual Traffic Offender: 3 major violations, or any combination of 12 major/minor violations, in 5 years = 4-year suspension, $500 reinstatement.
- [Ch.3, Suspensions — Criminal] OUI conviction license suspension: 1 year (1st), 2 years (2nd), 8 years (3rd), 10 years (4th), lifetime (5th). Leaving scene, injury crash: 1–2 years. Leaving scene, property damage: 60 days–1 year. Operating to endanger: 60 days–1 year. Motor vehicle homicide: 15 years–lifetime.
- [Ch.3, Suspensions — Fake ID] RMV may suspend 6 months for false/altered ID offenses even without conviction; 1 year if convicted.
- [Ch.3, OUI Penalties Chart] OUI 1st offense conviction: fine $500–$5,000, up to 2½ years prison, 1-year license suspension; court may allow alcohol education program reducing suspension to 45–90 days (over 21) or 210 days (under 21).
- [Ch.3, Failed Chemical Test] BAC 0.08+ = failed chemical test for all drivers; drivers under 21 face administrative sanctions at BAC as low as 0.02.
- [Ch.3, Failed Chemical Test] Failed test suspension: over 21 = 30 days or until case concludes, whichever shorter; age 18–21 = 30 days + extra 180 days (waivable on entering approved alcohol education program, 1st OUI case); under 18 = 30 days + extra 1 year (reducible to 180 days on entering program, 1st case).
- [Ch.3, Refusing a Chemical Test] Refusal suspension, over 21: no prior OUI 180 days; 1 prior 3 years; 2 priors 5 years; 3+ priors lifetime. Under 21: add 180 days (age 18–21) or 1 year (under 18).
- [Ch.3, Alcohol and Driving] Alcohol is a depressant: slows reflexes, distorts vision/judgment. One standard drink (≈½ oz alcohol: 12 oz beer, 5 oz wine, 1½ oz 80-proof liquor) can raise average BAC ≈0.02. Only time removes alcohol; coffee, cold showers, exercise, food do not speed it.
- [Ch.3, Implied Consent] Implied Consent Law: licensed drivers consent to breath/blood test in certain cases; officer may ask field sobriety test, and breath/blood test after arrest. BAC 0.08+ = over legal limit; under 21 zero tolerance at 0.02+. Over limit or refusal: officer takes license, suspension effective immediately.
- [Ch.3, Ignition Interlock] Driver with 2+ OUI offenses needs Ignition Interlock Device (at own expense) for hardship license / reinstatement; device used during hardship period + 2 years after reinstatement.
- [Ch.3, Under-21 Alcohol] Under 21: illegal to buy alcohol, have it bought for you, or possess/carry/transport alcohol unless accompanied by parent/guardian; license suspension 90 days–1 year; buying/attempting to buy = 180-day suspension.
- [Ch.3, Open Container] No drinking while driving; no open alcohol container in vehicle even if a passenger holds it; fine $100–$500; under-21 violators arrested, fined, license suspended.
- [Ch.3, Drugs] OUI laws apply to drugs incl. marijuana (driving under influence of marijuana is a criminal offense), prescription and over-the-counter medicines; combining alcohol with other drugs multiplies impairment.
- [Ch.3, Nonrenewal] License not renewable with unpaid parking fines, unpaid excise tax, outstanding warrants, unpaid E-ZPass/Fast Lane or Tobin Bridge violations, abandoned-vehicle citations.

## Chapter 4 — Rules of the Road

- [Ch.4, Speed Limits] Fundamental speed law: never travel faster than safe for traffic, road, weather/visibility, pedestrians/bicyclists — even below posted limit.
- [Ch.4, Speed Limits] Limited-access highways: 50–65 mph; smaller highways 55 mph or lower. Minimum speed: 40 mph on Massachusetts Turnpike; 20 mph in Boston Harbor tunnels (Callahan, Sumner, Ted Williams).
- [Ch.4, Speed Limits] Unless posted otherwise, unreasonable if over: 20 mph school zone; 30 mph thickly settled/business district; 40 mph outside thickly settled/business district; 50 mph on highway outside thickly settled/business district.
- [Ch.4, Speed Limits] Thickly settled district = buildings less than 200 feet apart for ¼ mile or more. Some communities set thickly settled limit at 25 mph unless posted otherwise (MGL c.90 §17C); signs posted at town/city entrances.
- [Ch.4, School Zones] School zone speed limit 20 mph; may use flashing yellow lights or posted hours.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signals] Steady red: stop; right turn on red allowed after complete stop + yield, unless NO TURN ON RED posted; left on red allowed only one-way street onto one-way street, after stop + yield.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signals] Flashing red = same as STOP sign. Steady yellow: stop if safe to do so. Flashing yellow: proceed with caution. Flashing yellow arrow: turn permitted with adequate gap, yield to oncoming traffic/pedestrians.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signals] Steady green: go after yielding to vehicles/bicycles/pedestrians in the road; never block an intersection; left turn on green: yield to oncoming traffic. Green arrow = protected turn in arrow direction.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signals] Signals blacked out/not working: treat as stop sign in all directions. Pedestrian signals: WALK / DON'T WALK; pedestrians already in crosswalk when countdown hits zero have right-of-way.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signals] Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacon flashing (amber): must stop for pedestrians. Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon: dark = proceed with caution; solid twin reds = stop; flashing twin reds = stop, then proceed if clear.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signs — Colors] Red = stop/prohibition; green = direction; yellow = general warning; black/white = regulation; blue = motorist services; brown = recreation/historic/scenic; orange = construction/maintenance warning; fluorescent yellow-green = school, bicycle, pedestrian, curve warnings; fluorescent pink = incident management; purple = electronic toll collection.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signs — Shapes/Types] STOP sign: complete halt for every vehicle; stop before crosswalk/stop line. 4-WAY/ALL WAY plaque: all approaches stop; first vehicle to stop has right-of-way. YIELD: slow, be prepared to stop, let vehicles/bicyclists/pedestrians go first; full stop if traffic requires.
- [Ch.4, Traffic Signs] Regulatory signs: red or white background; red circle with diagonal slash = prohibited. Warning signs: yellow / fluorescent yellow-green. Guide signs: green = directions/interchanges; blue = services; brown = recreation.
- [Ch.4, Railroad Crossings] Round warning sign in advance of crossing. Crossbuck (white X) marks the crossing; number of tracks posted below if more than one. Flashing red lights: stop at least 15 feet before light post/gate; stay stopped until gate raises and lights stop; never drive around a lowered gate.
- [Ch.4, Work Zones] Orange signs/devices mark work zones; first action on approach = slow down; obey police officer / civil flagger directions (officer directions override signs/signals/markings); speeding fine doubled in work areas.
- [Ch.4, Pavement Markings] White lines = same-direction traffic; broken white = lane change allowed when safe after signaling; solid white = edge of roadway / stay, cross only to avoid danger; double solid white = crossing not allowed. Yellow lines = opposite-direction traffic; broken yellow = may cross temporarily to pass when safe; double solid yellow = no crossing to pass, left turn crossing allowed when safe; solid+broken yellow: passing allowed only from broken-line side.
- [Ch.4, Pavement Markings] White diamond = special lane restriction (HOV/bus only). Sharrows = shared lane marking for bicyclists. Bicycle lanes ≈5 ft wide, solid white lines; motor vehicles may enter only when turning on/off road. Red pavement = transit (bus/trolley/light rail) lanes.
- [Ch.4, Signaling] Signal before: lane changes, turns, pulling away from curb, pulling over, entering/exiting expressway. Sequence: check mirrors + blind spot, signal, move. Hand signals required if electronic signals fail.
- [Ch.4, Turns] Signal turn at least 100 feet ahead; on highway at least 500 feet ahead. Turn from lane closest to target lane; do not swing wide; once started, complete the turn.
- [Ch.4, U-Turns] U-turn only from lane closest to center line; never where NO U-TURN posted; never at hill crest / near curve / anywhere visibility under 500 feet.
- [Ch.4, Highway Driving] Entering highway: yield to traffic already on highway, accelerate to match speed. Missed exit: never stop or back up; take next exit. Signal exit at least 500 feet before ramp. General highway speeds usually 45–65 mph.
- [Ch.4, Intersections] Blocking an intersection or crosswalk is illegal; enter only with room to clear completely. Turning drivers must yield to bicyclists going straight.
- [Ch.4, Right-of-Way — Pedestrians] Always yield to pedestrians in a roadway; yield to pedestrians already in crosswalk when light turns green; never pass a vehicle stopped/slowing for a pedestrian.
- [Ch.4, Right-of-Way — Uncontrolled] Uncontrolled intersection: yield to vehicle that has entered from your right / is coming from your right; yield at throughways.
- [Ch.4, Right-of-Way — Four-Way Stop] Vehicles go in order they stopped; first to stop goes first; ties: yield to driver on your right.
- [Ch.4, Right-of-Way — Left Turn] Left turn: yield to oncoming vehicles, vehicles already in intersection, pedestrians and bicyclists in your path.
- [Ch.4, Right-of-Way — Driveways] Entering a paved road from private road/driveway/unpaved road: stop, then yield to pedestrians, bicyclists, vehicles on that road.
- [Ch.4, Rotaries] Rotary traffic moves counterclockwise; yield signs at entrances; yield to vehicles already in rotary and to pedestrians; signal right when exiting. Big rotaries designed for up to 40 mph. Roundabouts: smaller; speeds 25 mph or less; yield lines + crosswalks.
- [Ch.4, Passing] Pass on left generally; may not exceed speed limit while passing; never pass using breakdown lane, shoulder, or sidewalk. Crossing broken yellow to pass requires clear view at least 400 feet ahead; return to right lane before oncoming vehicle comes within 200 feet.
- [Ch.4, Passing on the Right] Allowed only: vehicle ahead making/about to make left turn; one-way street; road with one-direction multi-lane traffic — and only when road clear and wide enough for 2+ lines of vehicles.
- [Ch.4, Being Passed] Driver being passed: slow down, stay right, do not speed up.
- [Ch.4, Move Over Law] Move Over Law (Acts of 2008 Ch. 418): approaching stationary emergency/highway/recovery vehicle with flashing lights — proceed with caution, slow to reasonable and safe speed; on highway with 4+ lanes (2+ same direction), move over to a lane not adjacent to the stopped vehicle if safe; if moving over impossible, slow down.
- [Ch.4, School Buses] Bus/pupil transport vehicle flashing red lights + extended stop sign: stop from either direction, remain stopped until lights stop / sign folds; first violation: $250 fine + possible license suspension. Exception: bus stopped on opposite side of divided highway with barrier.
- [Ch.4, Trucks] Trucks/buses on multi-lane highways use two right-hand lanes only. Fully loaded tractor-trailer may need twice the distance to stop. If you cannot see a truck's mirrors, you are in its blind spot / following too closely.
- [Ch.4, Trolleys] Do not drive within 8 feet of a trolley passenger step while trolley is loading/unloading passengers.
- [Ch.4, Funeral Processions] Processions have right-of-way at intersections (must yield to emergency vehicles / police direction); non-participants may not drive between procession vehicles, join, or pass on the right on multi-lane highway unless procession is in far left lane.
- [Ch.4, Parking] Leave at least 12-foot-wide clear roadway when parking; in business/residential district park within 12 inches of curb; leaving vehicle: stop engine, set parking brake, lock ignition, remove key, lock door.
- [Ch.4, Parking on Hills] No curb: wheels inward toward road edge. Uphill with curb: wheels outward toward travel lane. Downhill with curb: wheels inward toward curb. Always set parking brake.
- [Ch.4, Parking Regulations] No parking: within 20 feet of an intersection; within 10 feet of a fire hydrant/fire lane; in crosswalk, bicycle lane, bus stop (bus stop fine $100), taxi stand, loading zone; double parking; facing wrong way; on sidewalk/median. HP-DV space first offense $300; wrongful use of disability plate/placard minimum $500.
- [Ch.4, Bicyclists] Bicyclist may use full lane; ride with traffic direction; max two abreast (single lane on multi-lane roads); helmet required for operator/passenger under 16; no carrying child under 1 year; child 1–4 years or ≤40 lbs only in attached baby seat with harness.
- [Ch.4, Motorists Near Bicycles] After passing a bicycle, do not cut back right until safely past; opening a door into an oncoming bicycle: fine up to $100; use far hand ("Dutch Reach") to open door; bicycle boxes (green, before crosswalk): drivers must stop behind the box even when empty.

## Chapter 5 — Special Driving Situations

- [Ch.5, Moving Emergency Vehicle] Yield to emergency vehicle using siren and/or flashing lights: pull as close as possible to right side, stop until it passes (slow rolling not acceptable); never stop in an intersection — drive through, then pull over.
- [Ch.5, Moving Emergency Vehicle] Illegal to follow within 300 feet behind an emergency vehicle responding to an alarm. Illegal to drive/park within 800 feet of a fire; do not drive over unprotected fire hose unless directed.
- [Ch.5, Police Stop] When signaled by police: pull to right side and stop, stay in vehicle, keep hands in plain view, wait for officer to ask for license and registration. License + registration must be carried (Ch. 90 §11); $35 first-offense fine for failure to produce either; false name/address or refusal: $100 fine and possible arrest/criminal charges.
- [Ch.5, Skidding] Skid response: ease foot off gas, shift to neutral, do not brake, steer in direction of skid (rear sliding left → steer left).
- [Ch.5, Emergencies] Off pavement: hold wheel firmly, ease off gas, brake gently, steer gently back when safe. Blowout: grip wheel, ease off gas, brake gently, pull off road when under control. Brake failure (no ABS): pump pedal rapidly to build pressure; with ABS do not pump; downshift, apply parking brake gently. Stuck gas pedal: shift to neutral, brake, try to free pedal with foot. Head-on vehicle in your lane: slow down, pull right, sound horn.
- [Ch.5, Stalling on Railroad Tracks] Train coming: get everyone out immediately, run at 45-degree angle away from tracks in direction train is coming from, call 911. No train: listen, try restart, shift neutral and push off; if cannot move vehicle, call ENS number on crossbuck / metal box and give DOT crossing number.
- [Ch.5, Breakdowns] Divided highway warning triangles/LED flares: 10 feet and 100 feet behind vehicle. Two-lane/undivided: 10 feet behind, 100 feet behind, 100 feet in front. Tie white cloth to antenna/door handle (red cloth in snow); raise hood.
- [Ch.5, Crashes] Any crash: stop; never leave the scene. No injuries: Driver Removal Law — move vehicles out of travel lanes. Exchange name, address, license number, registration, insurance info.
- [Ch.5, Reporting a Crash] Report required for any crash with injury/death or $1,000+ property damage; written report to RMV within 5 days, copies to local police and insurer; reporting only to insurer is not enough.
- [Ch.5, Witnessing a Crash] Witness placing warning devices: 200 feet or more in front of and behind the crash.
- [Ch.5, Green Driving / Idling] MA law prohibits vehicle idling for more than 5 minutes.
