# Fact Sheet — Washington (WA) — FACTS ONLY

Source: Washington State Driver Guide, © 2025, Washington State Department of Licensing.
One fact per line. Tag = Guide chapter/section. No Guide sentences copied.

## Licensing — Ch. 1

- [1.1] Every WA license/permit/ID carries an assigned WA number, usually starting with WDL.
- [1.5] New resident licensed elsewhere: 30 days to get a WA license; also 30 days to register vehicle in WA.
- [1.5] New resident with expired out-of-state license: must pass both knowledge and skills exams.
- [1.6] Standard license/ID: NOT valid for domestic air travel, border crossing, or federal facilities (not REAL ID compliant).
- [1.6] Enhanced license/ID: meets REAL ID; valid for domestic air travel, land/sea border crossing, federal facilities; NOT for international air travel.
- [1.8] Instruction permit via driver-training course: apply as early as age 15, 1–10 days before course starts.
- [1.8] Instruction permit without a course: knowledge exam may be taken after age 15½; skills exam/license must wait until 18.
- [1.8] Instruction permit valid 1 year, renewable for a fee; knowledge exam score valid 2 years.
- [1.8] First license at 16–17 requires: permit held ≥6 months; approved traffic safety course (30 h classroom + 6 h behind-the-wheel minimum for out-of-state courses); pass knowledge + skills exams.
- [1.8] Practice driving for 16–17 first license: ≥40 hours daytime + ≥10 hours nighttime, supervised by licensed driver with ≥3 years experience; parent/guardian attests.
- [1.8] First license 16–17: no traffic violations/convictions in the 6 months before applying; no alcohol/drug offences on the permit.
- [1.8] Temporary license issued in office is good for 45 days; official license arrives by mail.
- [1.8] Age 18+ permit practice: supervising driver must have ≥5 years licensed experience.
- [1.8] Intermediate license (16–17), first 6 months: no passengers under 20 except immediate family.
- [1.8] Intermediate license: no driving 1 a.m.–5 a.m. unless with parent, guardian, or licensed driver age ≥25.
- [1.8] Intermediate license, after first 6 months (until 18 or 1 year, whichever first): max 3 passengers under 20 except immediate family.
- [1.8] Intermediate license: cell phone / wireless device use banned while driving even hands-free; only exception = reporting an emergency.
- [1.8] Intermediate penalty 1st violation: current restrictions apply until age 18.
- [1.8] Intermediate penalty 2nd violation: suspension 6 months or until 18, whichever comes first.
- [1.8] Intermediate penalty 3rd violation: suspension until age 18.
- [1.8] Intermediate exceptions: driving for agricultural purposes (any hour, with passengers); restrictions lift after 1 year of safe driving (no crash, no offence).
- [1.8] At 18, intermediate restrictions lift automatically; no new license needed until renewal.
- [1.8] Agricultural permit: for under-18 farm work in a specific area; no minimum age stated; does not replace an instruction permit.
- [1.9] Vision screening required before license issue; corrective-lens use is recorded on the license.
- [1.10] Motorcycle or commercial vehicle operation requires an added endorsement.
- [1.12] Two exams: knowledge exam first, then skills exam; after passing knowledge, 2 years to complete skills.
- [1.12] Skills exam: only applicant + examiner in vehicle (exceptions: service animals, sign language interpreters); vehicle must be licensed, registered, insured, safe; brake lights, signals, seat belts must work.
- [1.15] License may be renewed up to 1 year before expiry; license expired ≥8 years → retake knowledge + skills tests.
- [1.15] Minor alcohol/drug/firearm offence can delay licensing until age 17.
- [1.16] Address change: update within 10 days; name change must be done in person at a licensing office.
- [2.1] Selling/gifting a vehicle: file report of sale within 5 days, remove plates; buyer must transfer title within 15 days to avoid penalties.
- [2.2] Plates required front and rear, clean and readable; frame must not cover letters, numbers, or tabs.

## Alcohol, drugs, DUI — Ch. 1.15 / Ch. 3.1

- [1.15] DUI illegal in WA (RCW 46.61.502); DUI also covers being in physical control of a vehicle, even parked (RCW 46.61.503).
- [1.15] Implied consent (RCW 46.20.308): driving in WA = agreement to be tested if an officer suspects impairment.
- [1.15] BAC limit age 21+: 0.08%. Under 21: 0.02%.
- [1.15] THC limit age 21+: 5.00 ng per ml of blood. Under 21: above 0.00 ng.
- [1.15] Refusing a test: driving privilege loss 90–730 days, or until age 21, whichever is longer.
- [1.15] DUI conviction consequences can include: licence suspension 90 days–4 years, probationary licence, ignition interlock, proof of financial responsibility, alcohol assessment/treatment, re-testing + reissue fee, fines, jail, higher insurance.
- [1.15] Alcohol/drug offences stay on the driving record for life.
- [1.15] Open container: infraction for anyone in a vehicle on the road to drink alcohol / consume cannabis, or possess opened / broken-seal / partly used alcohol or cannabis; opened products may only be carried in trunk or truck bed, not in compartments accessible to the driver.
- [3.1] Alcohol effects listed: drowsiness, blurry vision, slower reaction, poorer concentration/scanning, difficulty multitasking, overconfidence.
- [3.1] Cannabis: recreational use legal at 21+, but driving after consuming any cannabis is illegal at all ages; no safe amount for driving.
- [3.1] Prescription and over-the-counter drugs can impair; a drug being legal/prescribed does not make driving on it safe; "operating heavy machinery" warnings include driving.
- [3.1] Polydrug use = mixing 2+ substances; effects can exceed any single drug; most common impairment in fatal crashes per Guide.
- [3.1] Fatigue impairs like alcohol/drugs; signs include drifting, frequent blinking, trouble keeping head up, missing signs/exits.
- [3.1] Fatigue countermeasure: pull off and take a 20-minute nap; plan stops about every 100 miles or 2 hours on long trips; most people need 7–9 hours sleep.
- [3.1] Aggressive driving = traffic offence; road rage = criminal offence; report in-progress road rage to 911.
- [3.1] Street racing: penalties can include fines, jail, vehicle impound; applies also to people who instigate or assist.

## Vehicle — Ch. 2

- [2.3] Minimum liability insurance: $25,000 one person's injury/death; $50,000 two or more persons per collision; $10,000 property damage. Proof must be carried.
- [2.5] Tire tread must not be less than 2/32 inch.
- [2.5] Headlights required from ½ hour after sunset to ½ hour before sunrise; also in rain, snow, fog, smoke.
- [2.5] Dim high beams: within 500 feet of an oncoming vehicle; within 300 feet when following a vehicle.
- [2.5] Brake lights: red, clearly visible from 100 feet.
- [2.5] Signal 100 feet before a move/turn; hand signals: left arm out + fingers down = stop/slow; arm straight left = left turn; arm bent up = right turn.
- [2.5] Chest should be at least 10 inches from the steering wheel (airbag space); headrest level with ears.
- [2.6] Every occupant must wear a seat belt or approved child restraint; illegal to drive/ride unrestrained (RCW 46.61.687 / 46.61.688).
- [2.6] Belt fit: shoulder belt across mid-chest/shoulder, never behind back or under arm; lap belt snug on hips below stomach.
- [2.6] Child restraint: up to age 2 rear-facing; ages 2–4 car seat with harness (rear- or forward-facing); age 4+ booster until belts fit properly, typically ages 8–12, size marker 4'9".
- [2.6] Children under 13 should never ride in the front seat (airbag risk).
- [2.7] Hand positions 9-and-3 or 10-and-2; one-hand steering with hand at 12 only when backing.
- [2.8] ABS does not shorten stopping distance; it preserves steering control during hard braking; pedal may shake when engaged.
- [2.13] Blind zones: adjust mirrors, do shoulder checks; if you cannot see a large vehicle's mirrors, its driver cannot see you.

## Drivers / distraction — Ch. 3

- [3.0/3.4] Holding any electronic device while driving is prohibited for all drivers regardless of age; hands-free use is permitted but still a distraction risk.
- [3.4] Distracted-driving fines: fines after the first violation can be doubled.
- [3.x] Driving while wearing earbuds/headphones in both ears that broadcast sound or cancel noise is against the law; a hands-free device in one ear is allowed if compliant.
- [3.5] Scanning: look ahead, to sides, and behind; check mirrors and over-shoulder before turns, lane changes, slowing, backing.

## Sharing the road — Ch. 4.1–4.9

- [4.1] Pedestrians and bicyclists have right-of-way at crosswalks/intersections, marked or unmarked.
- [4.1] Wait until a pedestrian clears your lane plus one additional lane; if pedestrian uses wheelchair/cane/guide dog/service animal, wait until they have completely crossed.
- [4.1] Interfering with a service animal is unlawful; do not honk at a pedestrian with a service animal.
- [4.2] School bus: red flashing lights + extended stop sign = stop; yellow = caution/slow. Passing a stopped school bus: fines doubled. Passing on red is unlawful.
- [4.2] Same-direction drivers always stop for a school bus on red; centre turn lanes may not be used to pass — drivers in all turn lanes stop.
- [4.2] Opposite-direction drivers on a multilane road need not stop only if there are 3+ lanes AND lanes are separated by a median or barrier.
- [4.2] Proceed only after lights stop flashing, sign is retracted, and the arm returns to the bus front bumper.
- [4.3] Yield to a transit bus signalling and pulling back onto the roadway in your direction.
- [4.4] Loaded truck at 55 mph with properly adjusted brakes needs 450 feet to stop.
- [4.4] Merge in front of a large vehicle only when both its headlights are visible in your rearview mirror; large vehicles may need 2 lanes to turn and 2 lanes in a roundabout — do not pass a turning large vehicle.
- [4.5] Motorcyclists get a full lane width; never share a lane beside a motorcyclist; allow extra following room in rain/slick conditions; motorcyclists may slow via throttle without brake lights and some turn signals do not self-cancel.
- [4.6] Bicyclists have the same rights/duties as drivers; may use the full lane; keep at least 3 feet when following/passing; pass on the left, slowly, never into oncoming traffic; move over a full lane when 2 lanes run your direction.
- [4.6] Bicyclists in crosswalks count as pedestrians — yield in marked and unmarked crosswalks.
- [4.6] Drive in a bike lane only when turning or crossing it to park; check for bicyclists before opening a door after parking.
- [4.7] Trains always have right-of-way; a train can need up to a mile to stop; never drive around a crossing gate.
- [4.7] Stop 15–50 feet from the nearest rail when: signals flashing, gate lowering/down, flagger signals stop, train approaching as immediate hazard, horn heard, or stop sign posted.
- [4.7] Vehicle stuck on tracks: get everyone out immediately. Problem on/near tracks: call the number on the blue-and-white Emergency Notification System sign and give the crossing ID; if no sign, call 911.
- [4.7] Light rail: leave at least one car length from light rail trains; never stop/park on tracks.
- [4.8] Slow-moving vehicle emblem: orange triangle on the back = vehicle designed for 25 mph or less; pass with at least 3 feet clearance.
- [4.9] Emergency vehicles with lights/sirens always have right-of-way: pull to the right and stop immediately; re-enter traffic only after it passes, in your original order.

## Traffic laws, signals, signs — Ch. 4.10–4.12

- [4.10] Drive on the right; keep right except to pass; left lane is for passing, carpool lanes are not passing lanes; do not pass on the shoulder; backing up on a shoulder or freeway is illegal; hitchhiking on WA freeways is illegal.
- [4.10] Officials directing traffic (law enforcement, construction, maintenance, school crossing guards) overrule signals; their directions must be followed.
- [4.10] Dead traffic signal = treat intersection as a four-way stop; yield to traffic on your right.
- [4.11] Red light: stop; after a full stop, right turn on red allowed unless a "no turn on red" sign is posted and there is room; left turn on red allowed only onto a one-way street under the same conditions.
- [4.11] Flashing red = stop sign: full stop, go when it is your turn.
- [4.11] Red arrow: no travel in the arrow's direction.
- [4.11] Solid yellow: light changing to red — slow and prepare to stop; if already in the intersection, continue at posted speed; never accelerate beyond the posted limit to enter/clear on yellow.
- [4.11] Flashing yellow = like a yield sign; treat as uncontrolled intersection, proceed with right-of-way.
- [4.11] Yellow arrow: turning light about to turn red; prepare to stop, yield to oncoming traffic.
- [4.11] Solid green: go after checking the intersection is clear; still yield to emergency vehicles and pedestrians. Green arrow: right-of-way in arrow direction.
- [4.11] No flashing green signals in WA (seen in British Columbia as pedestrian warning).
- [4.11] Freeway ramp meters work like traffic signals: stop at the white line on red, go on green.
- [4.12] Sign colours: red = prohibitive/restricted; orange = construction/maintenance warning; yellow = general warning; fluorescent yellow-green = school/pedestrian/bicycle warning; white = regulatory; green = guide/directional; blue = motorist services; brown = recreation/cultural/historical.
- [4.12] Stop sign: stop at the line, crosswalk, or corner; check all directions; yield right-of-way.
- [4.12] Yield sign: slow down; let traffic with right-of-way cross first.
- [4.12] Speed limit sign: maximum for ideal conditions; driving under the limit can still be ticketed as too fast for conditions; some roads have minimum speed limits.
- [4.12] Automated safety cameras record: running a steady red, failing to stop at a railroad crossing signal, speeding in a school zone; camera locations are clearly signed; school-zone speeding tickets are mailed to the vehicle owner.
- [4.12] Red circle with slash over a symbol = that action (e.g. left turn, U-turn) is not allowed.
- [4.12] Do Not Enter: square sign, white horizontal bar in a circle — cannot enter from that direction.
- [4.12] Wrong Way sign: you are travelling the wrong direction — stop and turn around immediately.
- [4.12] Shared centre lane: reserved for left turns from either direction (or permitted U-turns); not for through traffic or passing.
- [4.12] Work zone signs (orange): yield to construction personnel, vehicles with flashing yellow lights, and equipment inside the zone; fines double for offences in construction areas when workers are present.

## Intersections, turns, markings — Ch. 4.13–4.16

- [4.13] Every intersection is legally a crosswalk, marked or not.
- [4.13] Four-way stop: first to arrive goes first; simultaneous arrival → yield to the vehicle on the right; left-turning vehicles yield to straight/right-turning traffic.
- [4.13] Two-way stop: turning vehicles yield to vehicles going straight.
- [4.14] Turn into the lane closest to the direction you came from; with multiple turn lanes, stay in your lane through the turn.
- [4.14] Signal at least 100 feet before turning across oncoming traffic; check behind for bicyclists and yield before turning.
- [4.14] U-turns generally allowed in WA unless a sign prohibits; need clear visibility all directions; never U-turn on a curve or approaching a hill crest.
- [4.15] Roundabout: all entering traffic yields; travel counterclockwise around a raised centre island; design speed 15–25 mph; choose lane before entering and stay in it until exit; stop for pedestrians/bicyclists in crosswalks on entry and exit; look left and yield to traffic already circulating.
- [4.15] Roundabouts: trucks may use the truck apron and both lanes; bicyclists may ride as a vehicle (middle of lane) or as a pedestrian.
- [4.15] Traffic calming circle: counterclockwise flow around a painted/raised island; may have stop/yield on all, some, or no approaches; large vehicles may need to go clockwise.
- [4.15] Diverging diamond: traffic crosses to the left side guided by signs/markings; left turns onto the freeway are free-flow without yielding to oncoming traffic; cross back to the right side after the overpass/underpass.
- [4.15] Uncontrolled intersection: yield if a vehicle is already in it, entering a state highway from a secondary road, entering a paved road from an unpaved road, or turning left with oncoming traffic approaching.
- [4.16] Yellow lines separate opposite directions; white lines separate same-direction lanes.
- [4.16] Dashed white: lane ending / merge or exit; may cross if safe. Solid white: stay in lane unless a special situation requires a change. Double solid white: barrier — crossing illegal.
- [4.16] Dashed yellow centre: may use oncoming lane to pass if safe. Solid yellow: no passing across it. Double solid yellow: no passing either direction. Solid + dashed yellow: pass only if the dashed line is on your side.
- [4.16] Median may be 18-inch solid yellow marking with stripes; driving within/over/across a median is illegal.
- [4.16] Shared centre turn lane: never use for passing; do not travel more than 300 feet in it.
- [4.16] HOV lane: diamond symbol; must meet posted occupancy; motorcycles allowed in HOV lanes; separated by solid white line.
- [4.16] Reversible lane: overhead green arrow = may use; red X = may not; steady yellow X = lane changing direction, move out when safe; usually marked with double-dashed white lines.
- [4.16] Stop line: stop before the stop line or crosswalk at signs/signals.
- [4.16] Bike lane buffer (two solid white lines with crosshatching) is part of the bike lane; do not enter except for a legal turn when safe.
- [4.16] Sharrows = lane shared with bicycles; drive slowly, give at least 3 feet when passing.
- [4.16] Bicycle box: no right turn on red near a bicycle box; stay behind the white line until the box is clear.

## Zones and parking — Ch. 4.17–4.20

- [4.17] School zone speed limit: 20 mph; signs may state when it applies; cameras enforce (see 4.12).
- [4.17] Work zone: reduce speed even if no workers visible; increase following distance; obey signs until the End Road Work sign.
- [4.17] Emergency zone (roadside response with flashing lights — tow, utility, maintenance, law enforcement, fire, ambulance, etc.): either move over to a farther lane OR slow to at least 10 mph below the posted limit, and never faster than 50 mph in the zone. (Guide states no doubled fine for emergency zones.)
- [4.18] No parking: in an intersection; on crosswalk/sidewalk/bike lane; within 30 ft of a signal/stop/yield sign; within 5 ft of a driveway/alley/private road; within 75 ft of a fire-station driveway across the street; within 20 ft of pedestrian safety zones; on railroad tracks; within 50 ft of a railroad crossing; within 15 ft of a fire hydrant; more than 12 inches from the curb; double parking; facing oncoming traffic; on a bridge/overpass/tunnel/underpass; on a freeway shoulder except emergency.
- [4.18] Curb colours: white = short stops only; yellow or red = loading zone or other restriction.
- [4.18] Always set the parking brake, especially on hills; parallel-parked vehicle must end within 12 inches of the curb.
- [4.18] Hill parking facing up with tall curb: wheels away from curb until back of front tire touches curb. Facing down: wheels toward curb until front of front tire touches curb. No curb: wheels toward the edge of the road.
- [4.18] Crime: leaving a child under 16 unattended in a parked vehicle with the motor running; leaving a child under 12 unattended in a parked vehicle.
- [4.18] Disabled parking: fine for using a reserved stall without the required placard/plates or blocking the access aisle; do not hang the placard from the mirror while driving (obstructs view).
- [4.18] Illegal to park in an EV charging space unless the vehicle is connected to the charging equipment.
- [4.19] Unsecured load is illegal; tie loads with rope/netting/straps; tie large objects directly to the vehicle/trailer.
- [4.19] Transporting an animal outside a vehicle (e.g. truck bed) without a protective harness or enclosure is illegal.
- [4.20] Ferries are part of the state highway system; all road rules apply; ferry line-cutting is a traffic offence with a fine; bicyclists get priority loading on most ferries.
- [4.20] Beach driving only in Grays Harbor and Pacific Counties; beach = state highway; speed limit 25 mph; pedestrians/bicyclists always have right-of-way; enter only at marked approaches, drive on hard-packed sand only.

## Hazards — Ch. 5

- [5.1] Speed limits = maximum legal under ideal conditions; WA law also requires a speed safe for current conditions; slow for sharp curves/hills, slippery roads, pedestrian/cyclist/animal areas, heavy traffic, narrow bridges/tunnels, residential areas.
- [5.2] Space: keep at least twice your vehicle length ahead when near other vehicles (Guide guidance).
- [5.3] Merging: traffic already on the interstate has right-of-way; use the full on-ramp, signal, mirrors; zipper merge at lane closures = use both lanes to the merge point, then alternate one-by-one like a zipper; Guide credits it with improving flow by 60%.
- [5.4] Eye-lead practice: pick a marker about 15 seconds ahead and count to calibrate distance judgement.
- [5.6] Night: drive at a speed that lets you stop within headlight glow (usually 400 feet); look to the right edge if blinded by oncoming high beams; no sunglasses/coloured lenses at night or on overcast days.
- [5.6] Curves: reduce speed before entering; steady speed and gentle steering inside; accelerate gradually on exit.
- [5.6] First minutes of rain on a hot day are extra slippery (oil rises in asphalt until it washes off); bridges/overpasses ice before other surfaces; shady spots can be icy; black ice is a thin, hard-to-see coating.
- [5.6] Winter traction: chains, snow tires, or studded tires may be used/required; snowy roads need much larger following distance and reduced speed.
- [5.6] Skid: foot off accelerator; brake appropriately (ABS helps keep steering); steer in the direction the vehicle is moving until traction returns.
- [5.6] Hydroplaning: water builds between tires and road, traction lost; prevention = slow down on wet roads; if it happens: foot off accelerator, gentle braking, steady wheel, look where you want to go.
- [5.7] Vehicle trouble: get off the road if possible; flashers + headlights on; place flares 200–300 feet behind the vehicle; raise hood or tie a white cloth to antenna/mirror/door handle.
- [5.7] Tire blowout: grip wheel firmly, keep straight, slow gradually, foot off accelerator, do not brake hard, pull off safely.
- [5.7] Engine shuts off: grip wheel (steering gets heavy), steady brake pressure, pull off, try restart; if it fails, hood up, hazards on, call for help.
- [5.7] Stuck accelerator: eyes on road, shift to neutral, pull off when safe, turn off engine.
- [5.7] Headlights fail: hazards/signals/fog lights on, pull off as soon as possible.
- [5.9] Crash: never drive away; if injury/death, call 911 and notify law enforcement; move vehicle to the side near the scene; use flares/warnings; turn off wrecked vehicles; do not smoke near wrecked vehicles (fuel/fire risk).
- [5.9] Crash into a parked vehicle: try to find the owner; if not found, leave a visible note with date, time, and your contact information.
- [5.9] Exchange name, contact info, licence number, insurance company and policy number; file a collision report within 4 days if an officer does not file one (required by state law).
- [5.9] Injured people: call 911; do not move them unless in a burning vehicle or immediate danger; do not give them anything to drink, not even water; keep them warm with a blanket/coat.
- [5.9] Power lines on/around vehicle: stay inside, turn off engine, call 911, assume lines are energised; bystanders keep at least 35 feet away; if fire forces exit, jump clear with both feet landing together without touching the vehicle, then shuffle 35 feet away.
- [5.10] Traffic stop: signal, pull right when safe, engine and audio off, stay in vehicle unless directed, interior light on at night, hands on wheel, tell the officer where documents are before reaching for them; officer typically asks for licence, proof of insurance, registration.
- [5.10] Ticket: signing is required and is not an admission of guilt; refusing to sign can lead to arrest; follow instructions on the back of the ticket within 15 days or privileges may be suspended.
