# New Jersey — Fact Sheet (facts only)

Source: New Jersey Driver Manual (English PDF, MVC manuals page; Chinese PDF used as cross-check).
Manual as served 2026-10-25: cover year 2026; MVC listing edition label "Revised 09/25".
Rule for this file: one fact per line, tagged with manual chapter/section. No handbook prose.

## Chapter 1 — The New Jersey Driver License System

- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] Driver must carry: valid license/probationary license/validated permit + valid insurance card + valid registration card.
- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] Validated permit holder must be accompanied by an appropriately licensed driver.
- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] Address change: report to MVC within 1 week after moving (including moving out of NJ).
- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] Legal name change: report to MVC within 2 weeks.
- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] New NJ resident with out-of-state license: apply for NJ license within 60 days (CDL: 30 days) or before old license expires, whichever sooner; surrender out-of-state license on receipt of NJ license.
- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] Valid license from another country: may drive on it up to 1 year.
- [Ch1 — Laws Governing Driver Licenses] Applicant under 18: signed parent/guardian consent form required.
- [Ch1 — Enhanced Security and License Issuance Process] Licenses/IDs valid 4 years; under-21 cards are vertical format; temporary license/ID valid 30 days; cards mailed, delivery within 30 days.
- [Ch1 — Enhanced Security and License Issuance Process] Altering/fraudulently obtaining/presenting a license or ID: loss of driving privileges, fines up to $1,000, and/or imprisonment.
- [Ch1 — Types of New Jersey Licenses] Basic Automobile License (Class D): minimum age 18 (permit: 16); permit fee $10.
- [Ch1 — Types of New Jersey Licenses] Probationary Automobile License: minimum age 17 (permit: 16).
- [Ch1 — Types of New Jersey Licenses] Motorcycle License (Class E): minimum age 17. Moped License: minimum age 15. CDL: minimum age 18; under-21 CDL holders intrastate only, no Hazmat or passenger endorsements.
- [Ch1 — Identity Verification] Standard license: 6 points of ID total; at least 1 primary document + at least 1 secondary document + proof of NJ residential address; no more than two 1-point documents.
- [Ch1 — Identity Verification] REAL ID: "2+1+6" = 2 proofs of NJ residential address + 1 proof of full Social Security number + 6 points of ID.
- [Ch1 — Identity Verification] ID documents must be original or certified copies, unlaminated, in English or with approved translation.
- [Ch1 — Graduated Driver License (GDL)] GDL violation penalty: $100 fine or suspension of driving privilege.
- [Ch1 — GDL Option A, Early Bird Road] Special Learner Permit: age 16 minimum; signed parent/guardian consent; enrolled in approved behind-the-wheel course; approved instructor purchases the permit; must pass MVC knowledge + vision tests.
- [Ch1 — GDL Option A / Special Learner Permits] Approved behind-the-wheel course = 6 full hours in a dual-controlled vehicle; special learner permit valid 2 years; permit validated only after course completion, before practice driving.
- [Ch1 — GDL Options A and B] Supervised practice (under 21): at least 6 months; minimum 50 practice hours including 10 hours during darkness; Certification of Supervised Driving form (BA-CSD) presented at time of licensure.
- [Ch1 — GDL Option B, Young Adult Road] Examination Permit: age 17 minimum; parent/guardian consent if under 18; then 6 months supervised practice + 50 hours (10 in darkness) before probationary license.
- [Ch1 — GDL Option C, Adult Road] Examination Permit at age 21+: supervised practice at least 3 months before probationary license (no 50-hour requirement stated for this option).
- [Ch1 — GDL, all options] Probationary license: at least 1 year of unsupervised driving observing probationary restrictions; Basic Driver License at age 18 minimum after that year.
- [Ch1 — Special Learner Permit Restrictions] No driving 11:01 p.m.–5:00 a.m.
- [Ch1 — Special Learner / Examination Permit Restrictions] No hand-held or hands-free cell phones or any other electronic devices while driving.
- [Ch1 — Permit Restrictions] Supervising driver: front seat; age 21+; valid NJ driver license; minimum 3 years driving experience.
- [Ch1 — Special Learner Permit Restrictions] Passengers: limited to parent/guardian/dependents of permit holder + 1 additional passenger.
- [Ch1 — Examination Permit / Probationary Restrictions] Passengers: dependents of holder + 1 additional passenger, unless accompanied by a parent/guardian.
- [Ch1 — Permit / Probationary Restrictions] Permit holder and all passengers must wear seat belts.
- [Ch1 — Restrictions footnote] If Examination Permit or Probationary License holder is 21 or older: hour, passenger, and decal restrictions do not apply.
- [Ch1 — GDL Decal] Drivers under 21 with a permit or probationary license: display 2 visible red reflective decals, one on top-left corner of rear plate, one on top-left corner of front plate; decals $4 per pair.
- [Ch1 — GDL Driver Exemptions] Hours-rule exemption for employment/religion: carry signed certification on official letterhead of employer/religious institution.
- [Ch1 — Special Learner Permits note] Traffic offense by a student driver: responsibility shared by student and instructor/supervising driver.
- [Ch1 — Probationary note] Probationary holder must visit an MVC Licensing Center after the standard 12 months to upgrade to a basic license; failure to upgrade keeps probationary restrictions in force.
- [Ch1 — Examination Permits for Out-of-State Drivers] Knowledge and road tests waived with a valid non-probationary license from any of the 50 states, DC, or US territories; vision screening still required.
- [Ch1 — Non-Driver ID] Non-driver ID available from age 14; must be surrendered if the person later gets an NJ driver license.
- [Ch1 — Organ Donation] Organ donor registration possible from age 14 (with non-driver ID); "organ donor" wording appears on the license.
- [Ch1 — Next-of-Kin Registry (Sara's Law)] Registry open from age 14; contacts used by law enforcement only after a crash that leaves the person unable to communicate.
- [Ch1 — Hearing-Impaired Designation] Hearing-impaired designation: hearing loss of 41 dB or more; physician/audiologist verification required.
- [Ch1 — Commercial Driver License] CDL Class A threshold: combination weight rating 26,001 lbs or more with towed vehicle over 10,000 lbs; a valid basic Class D license is required before applying for a CDL.

## Chapter 2 — New Jersey Driver Testing

- [Ch2 — Test Requirements] Vision minimum, basic license: 20/50 with or without corrective lenses; sight in one eye only: that eye 20/50 + physician documentation.
- [Ch2 — Test Requirements] Vision minimum, CDL: 20/40 in both eyes with or without lenses; must distinguish red, green, amber.
- [Ch2 — Knowledge Test] Knowledge test = 50 questions + a survey question about organ donation.
- [Ch2 — Test Requirements / Sample test page] Passing: 80 percent = 40 of 50 correct.
- [Ch2 — Knowledge Test] Test offered in English, ASL, and foreign languages including Chinese; headphones for oral test if applicant cannot read; interpreter request at least 2 weeks in advance.
- [Ch2 — Knowledge Test / Road Test] Test results valid 2 years.
- [Ch2 — Road Test] Under-21 GDL: minimum 6 months supervised practice incl. 50 hours (10 in darkness) before road test; completed BA-CSD form required at road test appointment. Age 21+: minimum 3 months supervised practice.
- [Ch2 — Road Test Checklist] Road test requires: 6 points of ID; validated permit (not laminated); valid inspection sticker (or official card), registration, insurance for test vehicle; 2 red GDL decals if applicable; examiner access to foot/parking brake; all signals, brake lights, wipers working; licensed accompanying driver.
- [Ch2 — Road Test Checklist] No rental cars unless applicant is named as a driver on the rental agreement; autocycles and self-parking vehicles cannot be used for the test; no practice driving on the test course.
- [Ch2 — Elements of the Road Test] Tested items include: driving in reverse, following other vehicles, nearing corners/intersections, parallel parking, seat belt use, starting, steering, stopping at signs, stopping smoothly, turning, turning around, horn use, yielding right-of-way.
- [Ch2 — Reasons for Rejection] Vehicle rejection causes include: improper/expired/no inspection sticker; no examiner brake access; unsafe tires; missing seat belts (required on vehicles made after July 1, 1966); tinting on driver/front door windows; missing rear-view mirrors; broken/cracked glass; door/window not operating from inside.
- [Ch2 — Safety Pledge to Nikhil] Pledge points: stop completely at stop signs/red lights; stay alert, hands on wheel; talk safe (hands-free, no texting/handset unless pulled over); plan ahead with 5 extra minutes.
- [Ch2 — Voter Registration] Voter registration deadline: 21 days before an election.

## Chapter 3 — Driver Responsibility

- [Ch3 — Seat Belt Law] Driver and all passengers in a passenger vehicle must wear a seat belt.
- [Ch3 — Seat Belt Law] Failure by driver, front-seat passenger, or children under 18 to wear a belt = primary offense (officer may stop the vehicle for this alone).
- [Ch3 — Seat Belt Law] Driver is responsible for all passengers under 18; front-seat passengers 18+ are responsible for themselves; unbelted back-seat occupants 18+ = secondary offense (summons only if stopped for another reason).
- [Ch3 — Seat Belt Law] Exemptions: passenger vehicles manufactured before July 1, 1966; vehicles not required to have belts under federal law; physician-verified medical reason.
- [Ch3 — Seat Belt Law] Seat belts improve crash survival chances by 60 percent; survival chances 3–4 times better with belt + shoulder strap; fastening a belt takes about 3 seconds; hitting a dashboard at 30 mph ≈ falling from a three-story building.
- [Ch3 — Child Passenger Safety Law] Child under 8 AND under 57 inches (4 ft 9 in): federally approved forward-facing restraint with 5-point harness in rear seat, or booster seat in rear seat.
- [Ch3 — Child Passenger Safety Law] Child under 4 AND under 40 lbs: rear-facing restraint with 5-point harness in rear seat (or forward-facing harness system per the law's option b).
- [Ch3 — Child Passenger Safety Law] Child under 2 AND under 30 lbs: rear-facing restraint with 5-point harness.
- [Ch3 — Child Passenger Safety Law] All children under 8 ride in a rear seat; if no rear seat exists, restraint/booster may go in front seat, but a rear-facing seat may never sit in front of an active passenger air bag.
- [Ch3 — Child Passenger Safety Law] Violation fine: not less than $50, not more than $75, plus fees and court costs.
- [Ch3 — Air Bags] Air bags supplement seat belts in frontal crashes; inflate at speeds up to 200 mph.
- [Ch3 — Air Bags] Children 12 and younger are safest belted in the back seat; air bag markings: "air bag", "SRS", or "SIR".
- [Ch3 — Car Condition: Brakes] Braking benchmark: vehicle should stop within 25 feet at 20 mph.
- [Ch3 — Car Condition: Brakes] ABS: apply steady pressure; never pump ABS brakes or jerk the steering wheel while braking; on soft surfaces (loose gravel, unpacked snow) ABS may lengthen stopping distance.
- [Ch3 — Car Condition: Backup Lights] Backup lights on while vehicle moves forward = against NJ law.
- [Ch3 — Car Condition: Tires] Minimum tread: 1/16 inch (about the edge of a penny); do not mix radials with other tire types; check pressure when tires are cold.
- [Ch3 — Car Condition: Windshield] Add-on tinting on windshields and front side windows is prohibited.
- [Ch3 — Car Condition: Snow and Ice] Remove accumulated snow/ice from hood, trunk, roof, windshield before driving; violation fine $25–$75 even if nothing dislodges.
- [Ch3 — Carbon Monoxide Poisoning] CO is odorless and colorless; risk conditions: exhaust leak/poorly tuned engine, open trunk or tailgate, holes in body, idling in a garage/confined space, blocked tailpipe; symptoms: headache, dizziness, loss of consciousness, nausea/vomiting, chest pain, confusion; response: get the person away from the source and call 911.
- [Ch3 — Starting Checklist] Before driving: windows clean, seat adjusted, mirrors adjusted, belts fastened, vehicle in park/neutral with parking brake set, doors locked; outside mirror target: tip of driver-side front door handle visible in lower right of the mirror.
- [Ch3 — Starting Your Car] More than 95 percent of needed driving information is visual; eye checkup recommended every 2 years.
- [Ch3 — Idling Your Vehicle] Idling limit: 3 minutes or less; owner fines $250–$1,000 per violation; exceptions include being stuck in traffic and drive-thru establishments.
- [Ch3 — Steering: Hand Position] Normal grip: outside rim at 9 and 3 o'clock, thumbs along the face of the wheel; never grip from inside the rim; both hands on the wheel except when shifting or hand-signaling.
- [Ch3 — Stopping Distances] Stopping distance depends on: reaction time; weather/visibility; vehicle weight; brake condition; tire condition/type; roadway condition; speed.
- [Ch3 — Stopping Distances table] Total stopping distance (daytime table, 3/4-second reaction): 10 mph = 19 ft; 20 mph = 53 ft; 30 mph = 102 ft; 40 mph = 167 ft; 50 mph = 247 ft; 60 mph = 343 ft; 70 mph = 453 ft.
- [Ch3 — Stopping Distances] At 60 mph a vehicle travels 88 feet per second; typical reaction time 3/4 second.
- [Ch3 — Proper Braking] Use the right foot for both brake and gas pedals; left foot is for the clutch on manual transmissions.
- [Ch3 — Driving Signals] Signal at least 100 feet before turning, changing lanes, stopping, or slowing; cancel the signal after the turn.
- [Ch3 — Driving Signals] Hand signals: stop/slowing = hand and arm downward, palm to rear; right turn = hand and arm upward; left turn = hand and arm straight out; bicyclists/skaters may signal a right turn with the right arm straight out instead.
- [Ch3 — Driving Signals] Horn must be audible at least 200 feet under normal conditions; only emergency vehicles may use sirens, whistles, or bells; horn may be sounded when passing outside business/residential zones.
- [Ch3 — Driving in Reverse] Reverse speed: walking speed, about 2–4 mph; turn head/body to the right, right arm over the back of the front passenger seat, left hand at top of wheel; front of vehicle swings opposite the direction of the turn; road test includes backing about 100 feet in a straight line; backup cameras/sensors permitted on the road test (since Aug 31, 2016).
- [Ch3 — Turning] Missed turn: never back up; take the next turn instead.
- [Ch3 — Turning: 3-Point Turn] K-turn starts from the right edge of the road in a spot with good visibility both ways.
- [Ch3 — Parking] Parked on a hill: always set the brake; downhill = wheels turned toward the curb; uphill = wheels turned away from the curb.
- [Ch3 — Parking: Parallel Parking] Pull alongside 2–4 feet from the vehicle in front; when finished, tires no more than 6 inches from the curb; practice markers/flags 25 feet apart.

## Chapter 4 — Safe Driving Rules and Regulations

- [Ch4 — Speed Control] Speed limits unless otherwise posted: 25 mph — school zones, business or residential districts; 35 mph — suburban business and residential districts; 50 mph — non-posted rural roadways; 55 mph — certain state highways (as posted) and interstates; 65 mph — certain interstate highways (as posted).
- [Ch4 — Speed Control] Exceeding a 65 mph limit by 10 mph or more = double fines; most other moving violations in a 65 mph zone also carry double fines.
- [Ch4 — Speed Control] Basic rule: never drive faster than weather, road, or traffic conditions safely allow, regardless of the posted limit.
- [Ch4 — Speed Control] Always slow down: on narrow/winding roads; at intersections or railroad crossings; on hills; at sharp or blind curves; where pedestrians or hazards are present; on wet/icy/slippery roads; when visibility is limited.
- [Ch4 — Speed Control: Safe Corridors] Safe Corridor highways: fines doubled; designation based on crash rates 50 percent over the state rate and 1,000+ crashes over a three-year period.
- [Ch4 — Passing] Both center lines solid = no passing; one broken line = passing allowed only on the broken-line side; both broken = passing allowed on both sides; a pass must be completed before the lines become solid again.
- [Ch4 — Passing] Passing on the right allowed only: on roads with more than one lane in the same direction with vehicles in two or more substantially continuous lines, or when the vehicle ahead is making a left turn and there is room; never pass on the right shoulder (against the law).
- [Ch4 — Passing] Do not pass: on a hill/curve without enough view ahead; at a street crossing or intersection; at a railroad crossing; on narrow bridges, in underpasses or tunnels; where signs/markings prohibit; behind a vehicle stopped for a pedestrian.
- [Ch4 — Keeping to the Right] Drive on the right half of the road except when passing or on a one-way street; on multi-lane roads use the rightmost available lane except when overtaking or preparing for a left turn.
- [Ch4 — Yielding the Right-of-Way] Always yield to: emergency vehicles using sirens/flashing lights; trains at crossings; buses re-entering traffic; postal vehicles re-entering traffic; motorized/mobility-assistance devices in a crosswalk; vehicles already in the intersection.
- [Ch4 — Pedestrians in a Crosswalk] For a pedestrian in a marked crosswalk (or crossing at an unmarked crosswalk at an intersection): come to a complete stop; remain stopped until the pedestrian has cleared the driver's lane and the adjacent lane in the same direction; stop even if the signal changes to "Don't Walk" while the pedestrian is crossing; never pass a vehicle stopped for a pedestrian; never block or park in a crosswalk.
- [Ch4 — Pedestrians in a Crosswalk] Failure to stop for a pedestrian: fines up to $500, up to 25 days in jail, community service, license suspension up to 6 months, and 2 points.
- [Ch4 — Intersections] Single solid white line across a road at an intersection = stop behind the line for a signal or sign.
- [Ch4 — Intersections: Controlled] Multi-way stop: yield to the motorist on the right if arriving at the same time, and to any vehicle already stopped at the intersection; at a yield sign, slow down, give way to intersecting traffic, stop if necessary; left turn: yield to oncoming traffic and stop for pedestrians in the crosswalk.
- [Ch4 — Intersections: Controlled] Stop sign: bring the vehicle to a complete stop within 5 feet of the nearest crosswalk or stop line; enter only after yielding to pedestrians and to traffic close enough to be an immediate hazard.
- [Ch4 — Intersections] Driving on public or private property to avoid a traffic sign or signal = motor vehicle violation.
- [Ch4 — Intersections: Uncontrolled] Uncontrolled intersection: reduce speed and be ready to stop; traffic from a private road/driveway yields to all traffic on the main road; the vehicle on the left yields to the vehicle on the right.
- [Ch4 — Intersections: Uncontrolled] Traffic signal dark due to power failure/malfunction = treat as a 4-way stop.
- [Ch4 — Intersections: Traffic Circle] NJ traffic circles have no set right-of-way rules; historic flow pattern and entrance stop/yield signs govern; basic backup rule: vehicle on the left yields to the vehicle approaching from the right.
- [Ch4 — Entering Highways] Acceleration lanes are for speeding up to join traffic flow; yield to traffic already on the main road; avoid a complete stop in the acceleration lane; enter the right-hand lane when safe.
- [Ch4 — Leaving Highways] Deceleration lanes are usually on the right; if an exit is missed, go to the next exit; never back up on an exit ramp or deceleration lane.
- [Ch4 — Weave Lane] Weave lane = combined entrance + exit; the motorist entering from the entrance ramp must yield to the motorist leaving the expressway.
- [Ch4 — Disabled Vehicles] Own vehicle disabled: pull as far as possible onto shoulder/median; turn on emergency flashers; raise the hood; stay in the vehicle with seat belt on and doors locked; call for help; do not get into a stranger's vehicle.
- [Ch4 — Curves] Enter a curve by slowing down before entering; avoid drifting into another lane.
- [Ch4 — Right Turn on Red] Allowed after a full stop with a yield to all oncoming traffic and pedestrians, unless a No Turn on Red sign is posted.
- [Ch4 — Right Turns] Approach as far right as possible, close to the curb; do not swing outward or into another lane.
- [Ch4 — Left Turns] Two vehicles both turning left: each turns to the left of the center of the intersection; one-way to one-way: turn from the left lane into the left lane; two-way to two-way: keep to the right of the center line of the road being entered; two-way to four-lane highway: turn into the lane nearest the center line (the passing lane), then move right when traffic permits.
- [Ch4 — Stopping Regulations] Yellow light after green: stop before entering the intersection unless too close to stop safely; a "stale" green light has been green for some time — be prepared for it to change; most signal crashes happen in the first seconds after a light changes.
- [Ch4 — Stopping Regulations: Must Stop] A motorist must stop: at a stop sign; at a flashing or steady red light; at yellow after green (unless too close); when a traffic officer orders a stop; at a yield sign when merging is unsafe; for a school bus loading/unloading with red lights flashing; when coming from an alley, private driveway, or building; at a bridge span about to open for boat traffic; for a blind pedestrian with a white/metallic cane or guide dog (or a guide-dog instructor); for a pedestrian in a crosswalk; for a motorized wheelchair/mobility device in a crosswalk.
- [Ch4 — Stop at Railroad Crossings] Stop at least 15 feet from the crossing when lights flash, bells ring, gates descend/are lowered, or flag signals show; proceed only after gates are raised, bells stop, and red lights stop flashing.
- [Ch4 — Stop at Railroad Crossings] Most trains need more than a mile to stop when traveling at 60 mph or more.
- [Ch4 — Stop at Railroad Crossings: Special Circumstances] School buses, buses, and vehicles carrying hazardous materials must always stop at railroad crossings.
- [Ch4 — Stop at Railroad Crossings] Never stop a vehicle on railroad tracks; before crossing, make sure there is room to get completely across; if warning devices activate while crossing, proceed to the other side immediately.
- [Ch4 — Stop for School Buses] Stop at least 25 feet from a school bus with flashing red lights, on a two-lane road, a multi-lane highway separated only by lines, or a privately maintained road.
- [Ch4 — Stop for School Buses] On a dual-lane highway, traffic on the other side of a safety island or raised median slows to 10 mph instead of stopping.
- [Ch4 — Stop for School Buses] Bus light sequence: yellow/amber lights come on before the stop; red lights come on when stopped; proceed only after the signals are turned off, watching for children.
- [Ch4 — Stop for School Buses] Bus stopped directly in front of a school loading/unloading: a motorist may pass from either direction at no more than 10 mph.
- [Ch4 — Stop for Frozen Dessert Trucks] Truck flashing red lights with stop arm out: yield to anyone crossing to/from the truck; stop; then pass at no more than 15 mph; no stop required on the far side of a dual highway's safety island/raised median.
- [Ch4 — Emergency Vehicles] Steer to the extreme right, stop, and wait for the emergency vehicle to pass; keep at least 300 feet behind a signaling emergency vehicle; never park within 200 feet of a fire department vehicle in service; do not drive over a fire hose unless directed by an official; volunteer responders use blue lights.
- [Ch4 — Move Over Law] Approaching a stationary emergency/tow/highway-maintenance/service vehicle displaying flashing red, blue, amber, or yellow lights — or any stationary disabled vehicle using hazard lights, flares, or reflective triangles: change into a lane not adjacent to it if safe/legal; if a lane change is impossible, prohibited, or unsafe, slow to a reasonable speed below the posted limit and be prepared to stop.
- [Ch4 — Move Over Law] Violation fine: not less than $100, not more than $500; three or more offenses may bring motor vehicle points.
- [Ch4 — Using Headlights] Headlights required: from one-half hour after sunset to one-half hour before sunrise; when visibility is 500 feet or less; when windshield wipers are in use; in fog, mist, smoke, or other visibility reducers; parking/auxiliary lights may not legally replace headlights when headlights are required.
- [Ch4 — Bright and Dim] Bright (high) beam: open-country driving with no traffic in sight; glare recovery takes 3–5 seconds; at 50 mph a blinded motorist travels about a football field; look to the right edge of the road when blinded by high beams; dim (low) beams: city driving and traffic, when following or meeting other vehicles.
- [Ch4 — Parking Regulations] Parking more than 6 inches from the curb is illegal.
- [Ch4 — Do Not Park] Never stop/park: in a crosswalk; within 20 feet of the end of a pedestrian safety zone; near marked street construction; in a disability-marked space without authorization; on an interstate highway; on a sidewalk; in a bus stop zone; in front of a public/private driveway; within an intersection; within 10 feet of a fire hydrant; within 25 feet of a crosswalk at an intersection; within 50 feet of a railroad crossing; within 50 feet of a stop sign; within 20 feet of a fire station driveway entrance (75 feet on the opposite side); on a bridge/elevated roadway or in a tunnel; next to another parked vehicle (double parking).
- [Ch4 — Cellular Telephones] Hand-held electronic device use while driving on a public road/highway is prohibited and is a primary offense; hands-free use is permitted only if it does not interfere with required safety equipment or safe operation, and is strongly discouraged.
- [Ch4 — Cellular Telephones] Hand-held phone may be used only for emergencies: fire, traffic crash, serious road hazard, medical emergency, hazardous material emergency; one hand must stay on the steering wheel.
- [Ch4 — Cellular Telephones] Fines: first offense $200–$400; second offense within 10 years $400–$600; third offense within 10 years of the second $600–$800 + 3 points; a 90-day license suspension is also possible.
- [Ch4 — Cellular Telephones] GDL holders: no hand-held or hands-free phones or other electronic devices at all; school bus drivers: no phone use except in an emergency or when the bus is parked in a safe area off a highway.
- [Ch4 — Littering] Throwing trash/debris from a moving or parked vehicle is illegal; fines up to $1,000 for throwing "dangerous objects" onto a roadway; possible license loss if the vehicle was moving; carried loads must be covered.

## Chapter 5 — Defensive Driving

- [Ch5 — Prevent a Crash] Most crashes are caused by motorist error; crash-prevention formula: be alert, be prepared, act in time.
- [Ch5 — Aggressive Driving/Road Rage] Report an aggressive motorist: (888) SAF-ROAD, or #77 on a cell phone.
- [Ch5 — Distractions] Four distraction types: visual, manual, cognitive, auditory.
- [Ch5 — Tired Driver/Highway Hypnosis] Maggie's Law: knowingly driving while impaired by lack of sleep is illegal.
- [Ch5 — Tired Driver/Highway Hypnosis] Highway hypnosis: avoid looking at any one thing for more than a few seconds; rest every 2 hours and/or share driving.
- [Ch5 — Following Distance: One Car Length] Rule of thumb: 1 car length (about 20 feet) per 10 mph of speed; increase distance at high speed or in bad weather.
- [Ch5 — Three Seconds-Plus Rule] Count from when the vehicle ahead passes a fixed object; reaching it in at least 3 seconds = enough room for a sudden stop in good conditions; in bad weather increase to 4 or more seconds; on snow-covered roads use 6 seconds or more.
- [Ch5 — Following Distances table] Minimum safe following distance at 50 mph, ideal conditions: 5 car lengths; wet pavement at 50 mph: 10 car lengths.
- [Ch5 — Changing Lanes and Passing] Return to the right lane when the passed vehicle is visible in the rearview mirror; signal the return and cancel the signal.
- [Ch5 — Passed by Another Vehicle] When being passed: stay in the proper lane and slow down to make the pass easier; return to normal speed only after the passing vehicle is well ahead.
- [Ch5 — Wet Roads / Hydroplaning] Hydroplaning can begin at about 35 mph; above 55 mph tires may leave the road surface entirely, making braking and turning nearly impossible; reduce speed in heavy rain, standing water, or slush; drive on the highest part of the road (e.g., center lane).
- [Ch5 — Snow and Ice] A vehicle skids if the driver accelerates too quickly, turns too fast, or brakes improperly; studded snow tires permitted November 15–April 1; starting on snow/ice: keep engine speed low, use a lower gear if wheels spin; rocking = shift between forward and reverse.
- [Ch5 — Reduced Visibility: Fog] In fog: slow down; headlights on low beam; use fog lights if equipped.
- [Ch5 — Night Driving] Drive within the range of the headlights — always be able to stop within the distance visible ahead; nearly 90 percent of driving decisions are based on what a motorist sees.
- [Ch5 — Night Stopping table] Night total stopping distance (1.5-second reaction): 20 mph = 75 ft; 60 mph = 409 ft; 70 mph = 530 ft.
- [Ch5 — City Driving] In a city, look at least 12 seconds ahead; at 25 mph on a clear city road that is about one block.
- [Ch5 — Construction Zones] Traffic fines are doubled in roadway construction zones; warning signs/flashing lights may appear up to one-half mile before the work area.
- [Ch5 — Ignition System] Never turn the ignition to the lock position while the vehicle is in motion — the steering will lock.
- [Ch5 — Skids] Rear wheels slide: take foot off the gas; turn the steering wheel in the direction the rear of the vehicle is skidding, without oversteering; look in the direction you want to go; avoid braking during a side skid.
- [Ch5 — Emergency Stops] Place a warning device at least 300 feet behind the vehicle (about 120 paces); raise the hood; tie a white handkerchief to the antenna or left door handle if help is needed.
- [Ch5 — Running Off the Pavement] Stay on the shoulder and ease off the gas; after slowing to 25 mph or less, turn back with a one-quarter turn of the steering wheel.
- [Ch5 — Car Fires] Get passengers out and away from the vehicle at once and call for help; never attempt to put out a car fire yourself.
- [Ch5 — Plunging into Water] A vehicle with windows and doors closed floats about 3–10 minutes; escape through an open window while it is floating; a front-engine vehicle sinks nose first; follow air bubbles to the surface.
- [Ch5 — Stalling on Railroad Tracks] Get out and move far from the vehicle and tracks even if no train is visible; call the number on the Emergency Notification sign or 911; if a train approaches, run at a 45-degree angle away from the vehicle and tracks but in the direction of the train.
- [Ch5 — Brake Failure] Shift to a lower gear and pump the brake pedal fast and hard; if that fails, use the parking brake while holding the release so it can be eased if the rear wheels lock.
- [Ch5 — Tire Blowout] Hold the wheel firmly, keep the vehicle straight, take foot off the gas, do not use the brakes; coast to a stop in a safe area off the roadway.
- [Ch5 — Gas Pedal Problems] Stuck gas pedal: keep eyes on the road, shift to neutral, steer off the roadway, turn the engine off, call for help.
- [Ch5 — Hood Latch Failure] Hood flies up: slow down, look under the hood or out the side window, use center/lane lines as a guide, pull off safely.
- [Ch5 — Windshield Wiper Failure] Wipers stop in rain/snow: slow down, pull to a safe area off the roadway, turn on emergency flashers.
- [Ch5 — Avoiding Crashes] If a collision looks possible, the options are: stop, turn, or speed up; with ABS apply maximum brake pressure and keep steering control without pumping; a glancing blow is preferable to a head-on hit; prefer hitting something that gives way (bushes) over something hard; prefer an object moving in the same direction over a stationary one, and a stationary one over an oncoming one.
- [Ch5 — Rear Crash] About to be hit from behind: be ready to brake to avoid being pushed into the vehicle ahead; brace against the seat back with the back of the head firm against the headrest.
- [Ch5 — What to Do in Case of a Crash] Stop; remain calm; notify police and call an ambulance if needed; wait at the scene without blocking traffic; warn oncoming traffic as conditions require.
- [Ch5 — Reporting Crashes] Notify police of any crash involving injury, death, or vehicle/property damage; if someone was killed, do not move the body until police/ambulance arrive; when property damage is more than $500 or there is personal injury and no police report is filed, send a written report to the MVC within 10 days; notify the insurance company at once.

## Chapter 6 — Drinking, Drugs, and Health

- [Ch6 — Effects of Alcohol] Alcohol impairs reaction time, coordination, balance, vision, and distance judgment; after 2–4 drinks impairment of these abilities begins; only time can make a person sober.
- [Ch6 — Effects of Alcohol] Body eliminates alcohol slowly: about 90 percent is oxidized by the liver; the other 10 percent leaves through breath, urine, and sweat.
- [Ch6 — Effects of Alcohol] One drink ≈ 1/2 ounce of alcohol: 1.5 oz of 86-proof liquor = 12 oz beer = 5 oz glass of wine (12 percent).
- [Ch6 — How Much is Too Much] BAC is determined by: quantity of alcohol consumed; body weight; how quickly drinks are consumed; food eaten.
- [Ch6 — How Much is Too Much] Illegal in NJ: driving with BAC of 0.08 percent or higher; drivers under 21: BAC of 0.01 percent or higher.
- [Ch6 — How Much is Too Much] Crash risk: BAC slightly above 0.05 = risk doubles; BAC 0.10 = risk 6 times as great; BAC 0.15 = risk 25 times as great.
- [Ch6 — How Much is Too Much] Food slows alcohol absorption but does not prevent a high BAC and does not make a motorist sober.
- [Ch6 — Drinking and Driving] Telltale signs of a drinking driver: speeding; weaving; slow driving; jerking motion; quick stops.
- [Ch6 — Good Hosts] A host who lets a guest drive after drinking too much may become involved in a lawsuit if the guest crashes; hosts should offer alcohol-free drinks and food, stop serving alcohol well before the party ends, and arrange other transportation, a nap, or an overnight stay.
- [Ch6 — Designated Drivers] New Jersey was the first state to officially launch the Hero Campaign for Designated Drivers.
- [Ch6 — Drugs and Driving] Illegal to drive under the influence of any drug or to possess a drug without a valid prescription; drugs labeled "may cause drowsiness or dizziness" should not be taken before driving; cold pills, tranquilizers, and some prescription medications can affect driving skills; never mix drugs without medical advice; never mix alcohol with any drug or medication.
- [Ch6 — Drugs and Driving] After alcohol, marijuana is the drug most often found in drivers involved in crashes; marijuana effects include loss of tracking ability, impaired distance judgment, reduced vigilance, and divided attention.
- [Ch6 — Drugs and Driving] A motorist using prescription drugs must show proof of the prescription to law enforcement if asked.
- [Ch6 — Healthy Driving: Hearing] Never wear earphones while driving; keep the radio turned down so horns, sirens, and screeching tires can be heard.

## Chapter 7 — Driver Privileges and Penalties

- [Ch7 — The Driving Privilege] Driving is a privilege, not a right; a habitual offender is a motorist whose license has been suspended 3 times in 3 years.
- [Ch7 — The Driving Privilege] Suspension reasons include: failure to answer/pay parking tickets; driving while suspended; failure to provide proof of insurance; physical/mental disqualification; drug or alcohol use; traffic law violations; being at fault in a fatal crash; failure to respond to an MVC notice.
- [Ch7 — DUI] DUI = BAC 0.08 percent or higher, or operating while under the influence of liquor or drugs; under 21 with BAC 0.01 percent or more faces additional penalties.
- [Ch7 — DUI: Mandatory Penalties] First offense, BAC 0.08 to under 0.10: fine $250–$400; IDRC participation 12–48 hours; up to 30 days imprisonment; $1,000 annual surcharge for 3 years.
- [Ch7 — DUI: Mandatory Penalties] First offense, BAC 0.10 or more: fine $300–$500; IDRC 12–48 hours; up to 30 days imprisonment; $1,000 annual surcharge for 3 years.
- [Ch7 — DUI: Underage Offense] Underage first offense, BAC 0.01 to under 0.08: 30–90 day suspension of driving privilege; 15–30 days community service; IDRC or alcohol education/highway safety program participation.
- [Ch7 — DUI] Legal age to purchase, possess, or consume alcohol in NJ: 21; an under-21 person who buys or drinks alcohol in a licensed establishment: $500 fine and loss of license for a period of time; without a license, the suspension starts when first eligible.
- [Ch7 — DUI with a Minor] Parent/guardian convicted of DUI with a minor (under 18) as passenger: disorderly persons offense; suspension up to 6 months; community service up to 5 days.
- [Ch7 — Breath Test] Implied consent: by using NJ roadways, motorists have agreed to submit to a breath test after an arrest for an intoxicated-driving offense.
- [Ch7 — Breath Test] Refusal: the motorist is detained and taken to a hospital where staff may draw blood; refusal surcharge $1,000 per year for 3 years; refusal can also bring loss of driving privileges, IDRC referral, fines, and interlock installation (see Ch6 note).
- [Ch7 — Ignition Interlock Device] Interlock has a built-in breathalyzer and prevents vehicle start if the motorist's BAC exceeds 0.05 percent.
- [Ch7 — Ignition Interlock Device] First DUI offense: BAC 0.08–under 0.10 = interlock 3 months from restoration; BAC 0.10–under 0.15 = 7 months to 1 year; BAC 0.15+ = during suspension + 12–15 months after restoration; refusal = 9–15 months after restoration; second/subsequent DUI or refusal = during suspension + 2–4 years.
- [Ch7 — IDRC] Each of NJ's 21 counties has an IDRC; second-time offenders are detained at one of three regional IDRCs; offenders attend alcohol/highway safety education and are evaluated for treatment; referrals are to at least a 16-week treatment program; satisfactory participation is a condition for re-licensing.
- [Ch7 — Motor Vehicle Violations: Unsafe Driving] Fines: first offense $50–$150; second $100–$250; third/subsequent $200–$500; 4 points if a third/subsequent offense occurs within 5 years of the prior offense; $250 surcharge per offense.
- [Ch7 — Motor Vehicle Violations: Reckless Driving] First offense: up to 60 days imprisonment and/or a fine of $50–$200; points are also assessed.
- [Ch7 — Motor Vehicle Violations: Carrying Alcoholic Beverages] Open/unsealed alcohol containers (or unsealed cannabis items) in passenger areas: $200 fine first offense; $250 or 10 days community service for subsequent offenses.
- [Ch7 — Motor Vehicle Violations: Hit-and-Run] Hit-and-run with bodily injury or death: fine $2,500–$5,000 and/or 180 days in jail; first offense license loss 1 year; subsequent offense permanent license loss.
- [Ch7 — Motor Vehicle Violations: Lying on Application] Fine $200–$500 and/or up to 6 months imprisonment; license/registration privilege loss 6 months to 2 years.
- [Ch7 — Motor Vehicle Violations: Driving While Suspended] First offense: $500 fine + up to 6 months additional suspension; second: $750 fine + up to 5 days jail; third: $1,000 fine + 10 days jail.
- [Ch7 — Penalties table: No Insurance] Driving with no insurance, first offense: 1-year license loss; fine $300–$1,000; second offense: 2 years; fine up to $5,000; 14 days imprisonment; 30 days community service.
- [Ch7 — Driver Improvement Program] 12–14 points within a 24-month period or more = Notice of Scheduled Suspension; options: attend the Driver Improvement Program, request a hearing, or surrender the license for the suspension period; completing the program removes 3 points; $75 MVC administration fee; a 1-year probationary period follows completion or restoration.
- [Ch7 — Probationary Driver Program] Probationary period = 2 years after receiving a special learner or examination permit; 2 or more moving violations totaling 4 or more points in that period = must enroll in the Probationary Driver Program; completion can reduce the record by up to 3 points.
- [Ch7 — Defensive Driving Courses] Completing a defensive driving course removes 2 points; recognized for point reduction once every 5 years; may also qualify for an insurance rate reduction.
- [Ch7 — Violation Surcharges] 6 or more points within 3 years: surcharge $150 for 6 points + $25 for each additional point, paid each year for 3 years; DUI/refusal surcharge $1,000 ($1,500 for a third within 3 years); driving while suspended $250; no liability insurance $250; unlicensed driver $100.
- [Ch7 — Point System] Out-of-state moving violations add 2 points to an NJ record; up to 3 points are subtracted for each year without a violation or suspension, never below zero; point violations stay on the record (violations after March 1, 1974).
- [Ch7 — Moving Violation Point Chart] Points: tailgating 5; reckless driving 5; racing on highway 5; improper passing of a school bus 5; speeding 30+ mph over limit 5; speeding 15–29 over 4; speeding 1–14 over 2; improper passing 4; improper passing of a frozen dessert truck 4; improper right/left turn 3; improper U-turn 3; improper turn at a traffic light 3; hand-held cell phone use (third conviction onward) 3; failure to yield to a pedestrian in a crosswalk 2; failure to observe traffic signals 2; failure to keep right 2; careless driving 2; failure to yield to an emergency vehicle 2; Move Over Law violation 2; failure to give a proper signal 2; leaving the scene of a crash: 2 points (no injury), 8 points (personal injury).
- [Ch7 — Interstate Compacts] Nonresident Violator Compact: 44 states + DC are members; Driver License Compact: out-of-state violations become part of the NJ driving record; GA, MA, MI, TN, WI are non-members of the Driver License Compact.

## Chapter 8 — Sharing the Road with Others

- [Ch8 — People: Pedestrians] Vehicle-pedestrian crash fatality rate: about 5 percent at 20 mph; about 85 percent at 40 mph.
- [Ch8 — Crosswalks and Right-of-Way] A crosswalk exists at every corner of an intersection, marked or unmarked.
- [Ch8 — Crosswalks and Right-of-Way] Pedestrians must obey pedestrian signals and must not suddenly leave a curb into the path of a vehicle too close for the driver to yield; pedestrian errors do not remove the motorist's duty of due care.
- [Ch8 — Safe Passing Law] Passing a vulnerable road user with due caution means: move over one lane when possible; allow at least 4 feet of space when passing on a single-lane road; if 4 feet is not possible, slow to 25 mph and be prepared to stop until passing is safe.
- [Ch8 — Safe Passing Law] Penalties: violation causing bodily injury = $500 fine + 2 points; no bodily injury = $100 fine, no points.
- [Ch8 — Night Driving and Limited Visibility] Pedestrians walking on roadways without sidewalks should face traffic and wear reflective/light clothing at night; drivers must still stay vigilant.
- [Ch8 — Motorcycles] Check blind spots twice before changing lanes or turning; motorcycles can brake within shorter distances than other vehicles; failure to detect and recognize motorcycles in traffic is the most common cause of motorcycle crashes; when passed by a motorcycle, maintain speed and lane position.
- [Ch8 — Trucks, Tractor-Trailers, and Buses] No-zones are blind spots around large vehicles where the truck/bus driver cannot see other vehicles; in bad weather a large truck or bus can take 25 percent longer to stop.
- [Ch8 — Trucks: No-Zones] Rear: stay far behind a truck that is backing up; never pass close behind one. Front: do not pull in front of a truck after passing until the whole front of the truck is visible in the rearview mirror; never pass a truck on the right. Side: do not linger alongside a truck. Head-on: bear right when a large vehicle approaches from the opposite direction to reduce wind turbulence.
- [Ch8 — Trucks] Leave extra space when stopped behind a truck on an upgrade (it may roll back slightly); trucks may swing wide — e.g., swing left to make a right turn.
- [Ch8 — Yielding to School and Commercial Buses] All non-emergency vehicles must yield the right-of-way to buses re-entering traffic after picking up/dropping off passengers; once the bus is back in the normal flow, no yield is required for its lane changes; violation: fine $50–$200, up to 15 days in jail, or both.
- [Ch8 — Mopeds] Moped speed limit 25 mph; mopeds follow all traffic signs/signals and ride on the right side with the flow of traffic; moped drivers have the same rights and responsibilities as other motorists.
- [Ch8 — Bicycles, Skateboards, and Inline Skates] Bicyclists, skateboarders, and inline skaters have the same rights and responsibilities as motor vehicles and are classified as vulnerable road users; bicycles ridden after dark need front and rear lights and a rear reflector; a motorist turning right must yield to cyclists/skaters moving through the intersection; check blind spots before opening a door.
- [Ch8 — Motorized Scooters] Motorized scooters are illegal on public roads and sidewalks except on designated municipal/county property; they must be registered in the owner's municipality/county and insured; no one under age 12 may operate one.
- [Ch8 — Individuals with a Mobility-Related Disability] Mobility scooters for people with disabilities may operate on streets posted at 25 mph or less (up to 35 mph where local government allows); the scooter's maximum speed capability is 15 mph.
- [Ch8 — Low-Speed Vehicles] LSV = four-wheeled vehicle, attainable speed over 20 mph but no more than 25 mph; may not be gas- or diesel-powered.
- [Ch8 — Trains] Trains can travel on any track, in either direction, at any time, and always have the right-of-way; a freight train at 55 mph can take a mile or more to stop; trains can extend 3 or more feet wider than the tracks on both sides.
- [Ch8 — Trains] Do not pass other vehicles within 100 feet of a railroad crossing; do not shift gears (manual transmission) while crossing tracks; cross tracks only at designated crossings.
- [Ch8 — Animals: Horseback Riders] Horse-drawn vehicles and horseback riders have the same rights and responsibilities as motor vehicle drivers; when approaching/passing a horse-drawn vehicle, reduce speed to 25 mph or less and proceed with caution; on a hand signal from a rider/driver coming the opposite way, stop and remain stationary until the horse passes.

## Chapter 9 — Vehicle Information

- [Ch9 — Vehicle Title and Registration] A vehicle must be titled, registered, and insured before it is driven on public roads.
- [Ch9 — Vehicle Title and Registration] Moving into NJ: title and register the vehicle within 60 days (sooner if the out-of-state registration expires first); initial registration for a brand-new vehicle is valid 4 years; most other registrations are valid 1 year.
- [Ch9 — License Plates] Two matching plates for a passenger vehicle (one plate for trailer, moped, or motorcycle); plates mounted at least 12 inches and less than 48 inches above the ground; rear plate must be lighted and visible from 50 feet at night.
- [Ch9 — License Plates] Plate covers/holders that obscure lettering: fine up to $100; replace lost or damaged plates within 24 hours; forged/counterfeit plates: fine up to $500, up to 60 days imprisonment, or license suspension up to 6 months; surrender plates when a vehicle is sold without transferring them, or when insurance is terminated.
- [Ch9 — Vehicle Inspection] Gasoline passenger vehicles (model year 1996 and newer) more than 5 model years old: inspection every 2 years; new vehicles under 5 model years old get a dealer sticker valid 5 years; commercial vehicles: inspected annually; a new resident must have a vehicle 5+ model years old inspected within 14 days after registering it in NJ; inspection requires the driver's valid license plus the vehicle's valid registration and insurance documents.
- [Ch9 — Insurance] Liability insurance is mandatory for every vehicle registered in NJ; an insurance ID card (paper or electronic) must remain in the vehicle and be shown before inspection, after a crash, and when stopped by law enforcement; driving uninsured can bring fines, community service, license and registration suspension, and surcharges; if insurance ends, surrender the plates.

## Chapter 11 — Appendix: Driver Safety (signs, signals, markings)

- [Ch11 — Traffic Signs, Signals, and Road Markings] A police officer directing traffic can overrule traffic signals; law enforcement directions must always be obeyed.
- [Ch11 — Traffic Signals] Vertical signals: red on top, yellow in the center, green third, green arrow at the bottom; horizontal signals: red always on the left.
- [Ch11 — Traffic Signals: Red] Red light: stop before the intersection or crosswalk and remain stopped until the light turns green.
- [Ch11 — Traffic Signals: Amber/Yellow] Yellow light: stop before the intersection or crosswalk unless the vehicle is so close it cannot stop safely; a yellow arrow means the signal is changing from green to red.
- [Ch11 — Traffic Signals: Green / Green Arrow] Green light: proceed, yielding to pedestrians and to vehicles still in the intersection; before a left turn, yield to approaching vehicles; green arrow: proceed only in the direction shown by the arrow, yielding to pedestrians.
- [Ch11 — Traffic Signals: Flashing Lights] Flashing yellow: slow down and proceed with care; flashing red: stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, go only when safe; flashing red at a railroad crossing means a train is approaching.
- [Ch11 — Traffic Signals: Unlit Signal] A signal with no working bulbs and no one directing traffic = treat as a four-way stop.
- [Ch11 — Pedestrian Signals] Steady orange raised-palm: pedestrians must not leave the sidewalk/enter the roadway; those already crossing should move quickly to a safe spot; white pedestrian silhouette/countdown: pedestrians may cross; turning motorists must yield to them.
- [Ch11 — Sign Categories] Three categories: warning (hazards ahead), guidance (destinations/routes), regulatory (speed and movement).
- [Ch11 — Sign Colors and Shapes] White vertical rectangle = regulation; blue rectangle/square = motorist services and information; brown rectangle = public recreation and scenic guidance; green rectangle = direction guidance; orange diamond = construction and maintenance warning; red octagon (8 sides) = STOP signs only; red triangle (3 sides) = YIELD signs; yellow pennant (3 sides) = no-passing warning; yellow pentagon (5 sides) = school signs; white X = railroad crossing signs; yellow round = railroad crossing advance warning; yellow diamond (4 sides) = roadway hazard and railroad crossing warning signs.
- [Ch11 — Warning Signs] Warning signs: yellow, diamond-shaped, black symbol or word message; they warn of road conditions, deer crossings, railroad crossings, school crossings, and curved roadways.
- [Ch11 — Warning Signs: Advisory Speed] When the maximum safe speed around a curve or turn is lower than the posted limit, an advisory speed sign is used with the warning sign.
- [Ch11 — Road Work Signs] Road work signs: orange and diamond-shaped; they warn of lane closings, lane shifts, flaggers, uneven pavement, and detours; all traffic fines are doubled in NJ work zones; flaggers have the same authority as a regulatory sign.
- [Ch11 — Guidance Signs] Guidance signs identify destinations and routes; motorist service signs have white letters/symbols on a blue background.
- [Ch11 — Regulatory Signs] Regulatory signs are generally rectangular (longer vertical dimension) with black wording/border on a white background.
- [Ch11 — Regulatory Signs: Stop] STOP sign: octagonal; white wording and border on a red background.
- [Ch11 — Regulatory Signs: Yield] YIELD sign: white inverted triangle with red wording and border and a white border band.
- [Ch11 — Regulatory Signs: Cross Buck] Cross buck: X-shaped railroad crossing sign, black wording on white; it means the same as a yield sign — always yield the right-of-way to trains.
- [Ch11 — Regulatory Signs: Do Not Enter] DO NOT ENTER sign: white square; red circle with a white horizontal band across the center; words in white on the upper and lower parts of the circle.
- [Ch11 — Regulatory Signs: Truck Routes] Green signs mark routes/ramps where interstate trucks are permitted; red signs mark where trucks are prohibited.
- [Ch11 — Road Markings] Road markings have the same force of law as signs and traffic signals; yellow center lines separate traffic moving in opposite directions; white lines separate traffic moving in the same direction and mark road edges; a dashed line on the motorist's side means passing is permitted when safe; a solid line on the motorist's side means do not pass; double solid yellow/white lines prohibit passing; white dashed lines separate lanes on multi-lane highways; white diamonds painted in a lane = HOV lane; white stop lines show where to stop; white crosswalks mark pedestrian crossing areas.
- [Ch11 — Special Warning Signs] Slow-moving vehicle sign: fluorescent, reflective orange triangle displayed on the back of slow-moving vehicles such as farm and construction equipment.
- [Ch11 — Rumble Strips] Rumble strips alert a motorist to a decision point ahead; they vibrate the wheel and make noise; they do not damage a vehicle; they are almost always used with a cautionary sign.
- [Ch11 — Roundabouts] A roundabout is a one-way circular intersection where traffic flows around a center island; traffic enters and exits only through right turns.
- [Ch11 — Sign Chart (plates)] Sign names shown in the manual's sign charts include: Two-Lane Traffic; Sharp Turn Ahead; Divided Highway; Road Narrows; Winding Road; Merge; Hill; Lane Reduction Ahead; Cross Road; Cattle Crossing; School; Slippery When Wet; Hospital; Yield Ahead; Signal Ahead; Workers; Flagman Ahead; Detour; Road Closed; Stop; Left Turn Only; No Trucks; Do Not Enter; No U-Turns; No Parking Any Time; Do Not Pass; Advisory Ramp Speed; Side Road; No Passing Zone; Stop Sign Ahead; Bicycles Present; Bike Lane; Bike May Use Full Lane; No Standing Any Time; Reserved Parking; Stop Here on Red; No Pedestrians; No Turns; One Way; Keep Right; Wrong Way; Yield; No Right Turn; Stop for Pedestrians in Crosswalk; Railroad; Crossbuck (Yield to Train); Rest Area.
