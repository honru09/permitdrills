# Hawaii — Fact Sheet (facts only, no handbook prose)

Source: Hawaii Driver's Manual, English edition 2023 (HDOT), Chapters I–XIII + Definitions.
Cross-checked against the official Simplified Chinese edition (2021 printing) where noted.
Exam-format facts are tagged [Exam format — official county/HDOT page].
One fact per line. Section tags refer to the manual's chapters.

## Licensing (Chapter I — Licensing of Drivers)

- [Ch I] Minimum age for an instruction permit: 15½ years.
- [Ch I] Instruction permit: drive only when supervised by a licensed driver aged 21 or older; in non-motorcycle vehicles the supervisor sits in the right front seat.
- [Ch I] Permit holders driving between 11:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m. must be accompanied by a parent or guardian.
- [Ch I] An instruction permit must be held at least 180 days before applying for a provisional license.
- [Ch I] Minimum age for a provisional license: 16 years.
- [Ch I] Provisional license requires a state-certified driver education course (classroom + behind-the-wheel certificates) and passing a road test.
- [Ch I] Provisional license: may carry only one passenger under 18 who is not a household member.
- [Ch I] Provisional license, 11:00 p.m.–5:00 a.m.: drive only with a licensed parent/guardian, except driving to/from employment or a school-authorized activity; in those hours no more than one passenger under 18 unless a parent/guardian is present.
- [Ch I] A provisional license must be held at least 6 months before applying for a full driver's license.
- [Ch I] Minimum age for a Class 3 driver's license: 17 years.
- [Ch I] Applicants under 18 need consent of parent(s)/guardian (unless married or emancipated) and must hold the permit 180 days (age 16–17 without that holding period cannot be licensed).
- [Ch I] Under-18 permit and provisional stages: all occupants must use safety belts or child restraints.
- [Ch I] License classes: Class 1 = mopeds; Class 2 = motorcycles and motor scooters; Class 3 = any single vehicle with GVWR of 18,000 lb or less, and vehicles designed to transport 15 or fewer passengers including the driver; Class 4 = single vehicle GVWR 18,001–26,000 lb.
- [Ch I] CDL classes: A = combination vehicles GVWR 26,001 lb or more with towed unit over 10,000 lb; B = single vehicle GVWR 26,001 lb or more (towed unit not over 10,000 lb); C = vehicles designed for 16 or more passengers, or hauling placarded hazardous materials.
- [Ch I] CDL endorsements: H = hazardous materials; T = double/triple trailers; P = vehicles designed for 16 or more passengers including driver; N = tank vehicles; X = tank vehicles hauling placarded hazardous materials; S = school bus.
- [Ch I] Moped (Class 1) and motorcycle (Class 2) licenses each require a special knowledge test and an off-street skills test.
- [Ch I] Larger-class (Category 4 / CDL-type) licenses: applicant must be at least 21 years old.
- [Ch I] Applying for a license or permit involves: a vision screening, a rules-of-the-road knowledge test, and a traffic-control-device recognition element; the road (performance) test may be waived for Class 1–3 applicants who hold a provisional license or a valid license from another US state/territory, Canada, or a country with a reciprocal agreement with Hawaii; renewal applicants may also be waived.
- [Ch I] Road test: come with a licensed driver; bring a vehicle in safe operating condition with current registration, current safety inspection certificate, and insurance ID card; no passengers are allowed during the test; defects must be fixed before testing.
- [Ch I] An autocycle may be driven on a Class 3 license but may not be used for the Class 3 performance test.
- [Ch I] Category 3 test covers vehicles of 18,000 lb GVWR or less and buses designed for 15 or fewer occupants.
- [Ch I] Renewal: a license may be renewed within 6 months before its expiration date; every Hawaii license is void after the expiration date — there is no grace period; renewal is possible within 1 year after expiration, after which the original-license procedure applies.
- [Ch I] Renewal requires passing a vision test; a license may be renewed by mail once, but the driver must appear in person at every other renewal (REAL ID: in person at least every 16 years for an updated photo).
- [Ch I] REAL ID application documents: proof of legal name, date of birth, legal presence, Social Security number, and two documents proving principal residence address in Hawaii. A limited purpose license (driving only, not valid for federal ID purposes) is available to applicants who cannot provide a Social Security number.
- [Ch I] A valid license from another US state, DC, Puerto Rico, US Virgin Islands, American Samoa, Guam, a Canadian province, or CNMI is exempt from needing a Hawaii license; compact foreign licenses with an International Driving Permit are valid for 1 year from entry into the USA.
- [Ch I] A non-resident aged 21+ with a valid CDL from another US state, DC, a Canadian province, or Mexico is exempt.
- [Ch I] Exempt without a Hawaii license: federal employees driving federal non-commercial vehicles with a federal license; persons aged 13+ operating road machines/farm tractors temporarily on the highway between terminals.
- [Ch I] Cannot be licensed: during a court-ordered suspension/revocation; for 2 years after a conviction for drunk driving while unlicensed; without passing the required exams; if in violation of financial responsibility laws; if the examiner has good cause to believe a physical/mental disability makes driving unsafe; with outstanding traffic citations.
- [Ch I] Applicant information is checked against the National Driver Register; if NDR shows a license suspended or revoked in another state or DC, a Hawaii license will not be issued.
- [Ch I] The license card shows license number, birth date, issue/expiration dates, physical characteristics, class and endorsements, restrictions (e.g. corrective lenses), under-21 status, commercial status, organ-donor and advance-healthcare-directive indicators.

## Vehicle equipment, inspection, registration (Chapters II–III)

- [Ch II] Required equipment includes brakes, steering, horn, door handles, tires, exhaust, lights, glass, windshield wipers, odometer, speedometer, bumpers, wheels and lug nuts, fenders, floor pans, hood and catches, and gas tank, all meeting standards.
- [Ch II] Prohibited equipment: a red light showing from the front or a blue light visible outside the vehicle (except authorized emergency vehicles); a bell, exhaust whistle or siren (except emergency vehicles); a muffler cut-out/by-pass or a muffler louder than the original manufacturer's.
- [Ch II] Periodic Motor Vehicle Inspection (safety inspection) is required at least once a year; ambulances every 6 months; a new vehicle needs no inspection until 2 years after its first inspection at first sale.
- [Ch II] A Hawaii auto insurance ID card is required to pass the safety inspection; the inspection certificate must remain in the vehicle and a sticker is affixed.
- [Ch II] The driver is responsible for the condition of the vehicle being driven, even if not the owner.
- [Ch II] Tires: check at least once a month; replace tires with less than 1/16 inch of tread (penny test: penny upside down in the tread — if the top of Lincoln's head is visible, replace the tire).
- [Ch II] Steering free play: no more than 2 inches with power steering (engine running), 3 inches without.
- [Ch II] Brake check: applying the brakes firmly at 4–8 mph should stop the vehicle without pulling to either side.
- [Ch II] All front and back seat occupants must use seat belts by law (§291-11.6, HRS); children under age 4 must ride in a child restraint; children under age 8 must be belted on a booster seat when they meet the manual's size conditions.
- [Ch II] Air bags are supplemental protection for a first impact only; because they deflate immediately, occupants must rely on belts for any later impacts.
- [Ch III] A vehicle must be registered in the county where it is operated and licensed (registered) each year by that county; a current safety inspection certificate and proof of insurance are required before registration (proof of liability for motorcycles).
- [Ch III] Registrations are staggered and expire annually at the end of the 12th month; the registration certificate must be kept in the vehicle; the certificate of title is held by the legal owner or lien holder until the lien is paid off.
- [Ch III] Registration renewal by mail: send the application with payment within 10 working days before the end of the expiration month.
- [Ch III] Change of address or name of the registered owner: notify the county agency in writing within 30 days (name change: appear in person with proof).
- [Ch III] First registration in Hawaii includes inspection and weighing to record the vehicle identification number and weight.

## The driver (Chapter IV — The Driver)

- [Ch IV] Every driving situation has three elements: the environment, the vehicle, and the driver; only the driver gathers information and acts, so judgment and attitude are decisive.
- [Ch IV] Worry, anger, grief, fear, illness or overconfidence make a driver temporarily crash-prone; if worried, ill, nervous, frightened, angry or depressed, let someone else drive.
- [Ch IV] Drivers under 20 are involved in about twice the expected number of crashes for their group; drivers aged about 50–54 have the fewest crashes.
- [Ch IV] A mature driver declines all challenges to prove their own or their vehicle's capabilities; refusing a challenge shows maturity and responsibility.
- [Ch IV] Older drivers should keep fit, have annual physical exams, know how medication affects driving, drive within their limits, and follow a physician's advice about driving.

## Traffic laws (Chapter V — Traffic Laws, Ordinances, Rules and Regulations)

- [Ch V] Drivers must obey all traffic laws, all lawful orders of a police officer, and all traffic signs, signals and pavement markings.
- [Ch V] Drive on the right half of the roadway; opposing traffic passes on the right; on narrow roads allow opposing vehicles at least one-half of the main travelled portion; slower traffic uses the extreme right lane.
- [Ch V] Driving on the left half is allowed only: to overtake on a 2- or 3-lane road with no oncoming traffic; when an obstruction blocks your side and oncoming vehicles are not using the left half; on a one-way street.
- [Ch V] Never drive on the left half: on roads with 4 or more moving-traffic lanes (unless signs direct otherwise); approaching a hilltop or on a curve; within 100 feet of, or passing through, an intersection or railroad crossing; within 100 feet of a bridge or tunnel when you cannot see oncoming vehicles.
- [Ch V] Passing on the right is allowed: when the vehicle ahead is about to turn left (but never by driving off the pavement/main travelled portion); on a paved road with 2+ lanes each direction; on a one-way street with 2+ lanes.
- [Ch V] One-way roadways: drive only in the direction the posted signs indicate.
- [Ch V] On laned roads: stay entirely within your lane; do not change lanes until you have checked it can be done safely; use the lane indicated by signs/markings.
- [Ch V] Following: never follow closer than is reasonable and prudent for speed, traffic, road and weather; when towing, leave enough space ahead for an overtaking vehicle to fit in safely.
- [Ch V] Divided highways: do not drive across or through a barrier or unpaved dividing space except at a provided opening/paved surface where signs do not prohibit it.
- [Ch V] Signals: signal every time you slow, turn or change lanes, even with no other vehicles in sight; signal for at least 100 feet (about five car lengths) before the maneuver; use hand signals if the vehicle's signals are not working; signaling does not give you the right to make the maneuver; do not rest your foot on the brake pedal when not braking.
- [Ch V] Railroad crossings — stop no less than 15 feet from the nearest rail when: a warning signal device is operating; a gate is lowered or a flagger signals; a train within 1,500 feet gives an audible warning; or an approaching train is visible and so close as to be a hazard.
- [Ch V] Basic speed law: never drive faster than is reasonable and prudent; choose a safe speed for the area, road type and condition, traffic, pedestrians, weather and light.
- [Ch V] Never exceed a posted maximum limit; never drive below a posted minimum limit; never drive so slowly that you hold back traffic — if you cannot keep up, pull off and let traffic pass or take another route; on multi-lane roads, slower drivers use the right-hand lane.
- [Ch V] No racing, speed contests or speed exhibitions on public streets or highways.
- [Ch V] Reduced speeds are required near schools and playgrounds and at "School Crossing" locations when children are present, and on streets in residential or business areas. (The manual states no numeric limit for these areas.)
- [Ch V] Parking is prohibited: where hazardous to traffic; on a sidewalk; blocking a driveway; at/near an intersection so as to interfere with traffic; blocking a fire hydrant; on/near a crosswalk so as to block drivers' view of pedestrians; where signs/markings prohibit standing or parking; on a bridge, elevated structure or in a tunnel; double parking; more than 12 inches from the curb; in a passenger/freight loading zone; where less than 10 feet of street width remains for traffic; on a public road for repair (except emergency), washing, or display for sale; where the vehicle extends outside a marked space (unless the vehicle is larger than the space); on a public street/highway continuously for more than 24 hours; in an accessible (disability) space without the issued placard/plate for the disabled person being transported; in the access aisle beside an accessible space.
- [Ch V] Tow-away zones: do not park or stop, even momentarily (unless blocked by traffic), during the hours on the official signs; police will tow the vehicle.
- [Ch V] Leaving a vehicle unattended: stop the engine, lock the ignition and remove the key, set the parking brake, and on a grade turn the wheels toward the curb.
- [Ch V] More than three persons in the front seat may constitute prohibited interference with the driver.
- [Ch V] Emergency vehicles: on hearing a siren/bell/horn or seeing flashing red or blue lights — reduce speed, locate the vehicle, move out of its path and stop; do not panic-brake; if it is directly behind you in heavy traffic, keep moving slowly until you can get out of the way.
- [Ch V] Approaching a stopped emergency vehicle with lights flashing: slow down or stop as appropriate and, if possible and safe, leave an empty traffic lane between you and it (§291C-27, HRS).
- [Ch V] Funeral processions and convoys have the right-of-way at intersections; do not drive between the vehicles of a funeral procession; procession vehicles use headlamps for identification, with distinctive markings on the first and last vehicles.
- [Ch V] Slow-moving vehicle emblem: a reflective 18-inch triangular emblem displayed at the rear of vehicles designed to operate at 25 mph or less; on seeing it, slow down and approach cautiously.
- [Ch V] No passing: on the right shoulder; approaching a hill or curve without 700–1,000 feet of clear distance ahead; when the pass cannot be finished without endangering oncoming traffic and before the yellow line appears in your lane; where there is a solid yellow line in your lane; when a school bus on the same roadway has its flashing lights operating; within 100 feet of, or while traversing, an intersection or railroad crossing; within 100 feet of a posted narrow bridge, viaduct or tunnel.
- [Ch V] When being passed, it is unlawful to increase your speed.
- [Ch V] The end of a no-passing zone does not by itself mean passing is safe; it means visibility ahead has increased.
- [Ch V] Right-of-way at intersections: yield to the vehicle on your right when vehicles arrive at the same time; when turning left, yield to all oncoming traffic close enough to be a hazard; yield to traffic on the protected route (stop and yield where required); when the light turns green, yield to pedestrians and vehicles still in the intersection; stop and yield when entering a public street from an alley, driveway or farmyard.

## Signs, signals, markings (Chapter VI — Signals, Signs and Lane Markings)

- [Ch VI] Three device categories: regulatory devices (action required; failure to comply is penalized), warning devices (hazard ahead), guide/information signs (directions, locations, services).
- [Ch VI] Yellow centerlines separate traffic moving in opposite directions; white lines separate lanes moving in the same direction.
- [Ch VI] Single broken yellow centerline: drive to its right; you may cross it to pass when safe.
- [Ch VI] Double solid yellow lines: passing is prohibited in both directions; cross only to turn left into or from an alley, private road or driveway. A single solid yellow no-passing line may likewise be crossed only for such a left turn.
- [Ch VI] Single dashed white lane lines may be crossed when safe; a single solid white line means lane movement is hazardous (cross only in unusual circumstances, with great care); double solid white lines must never be crossed.
- [Ch VI] Red pavement markings or reflectors mark a roadway that must not be entered or used.
- [Ch VI] Edgelines: white along the right edge of the roadway; yellow may mark the left edge on divided streets/highways.
- [Ch VI] Stop lines are solid white lines across the lanes at intersections and crosswalks marking exactly where to stop.
- [Ch VI] Crosswalk lines show where pedestrians cross; drivers must stop for pedestrians in crosswalks.
- [Ch VI] Two-way left-turn channelization markings (solid + dashed yellow): the center lane may be used by traffic from either direction for left turns only, never for passing.
- [Ch VI] Obstruction markings guide you around obstructions such as bridge supports: you may pass on either side of white obstruction markings, but only to the right of yellow ones.
- [Ch VI] Painted directional arrows/words: travel only in the direction indicated; if more than one direction is shown, any indicated direction may be chosen.
- [Ch VI] Railroad crossing pavement markings: a large "X" with two "R"s, a solid yellow centerline (no passing), and a white stop line.
- [Ch VI] Traffic islands (safety islands protect waiting pedestrians): never drive into or across a traffic island.
- [Ch VI] Red light: stop at the stop line; if none, before the crosswalk; if neither, before entering the intersection; remain stopped until green.
- [Ch VI] Right turn on red is allowed after a complete stop unless a sign prohibits it; yield to all pedestrians and vehicles while turning.
- [Ch VI] Left turn on red is allowed only from a one-way street into a one-way street where traffic moves to the left, after stopping, unless a sign prohibits it.
- [Ch VI] Yellow light: the red light is about to appear; avoid entering the intersection on yellow; never enter an intersection after the red light is shown.
- [Ch VI] Green light: go straight or turn unless signs prohibit turns; yield to pedestrians and vehicles already in the intersection.
- [Ch VI] Arrows: a red arrow has the same meaning as a red light for that lane; a yellow arrow the same as a yellow light; a green arrow allows movement only in the direction the arrow indicates.
- [Ch VI] Flashing red signal = same meaning as a stop sign (a stop sign is also posted there); flashing yellow = proceed only with caution.
- [Ch VI] Lane control signals: red "X" = do not use that lane (it is used by opposing traffic); green arrow = you may use the lane; yellow "X" = move out of the lane because it will be used by opposing traffic; a flashing yellow "X" allows use of the lane only to prepare for a left turn.
- [Ch VI] Pedestrian signals: steady DON'T WALK / upraised palm = do not enter the roadway; flashing DON'T WALK / palm = do not start crossing, but those already crossing may continue to the nearest sidewalk, island or safety zone; steady WALK / walking person = pedestrians may cross toward the signal; flashing WALK = possible conflict with vehicles.
- [Ch VI] Sign colors: red = an action is prohibited; yellow = warning of hazardous conditions (almost always diamond-shaped); orange = road work warning (rectangular or diamond); black and white = required action (always rectangular); green, blue and brown = guide and information signs (mostly rectangular).
- [Ch VI] Stop sign: 8-sided (octagonal), white on red; other traffic has the right-of-way; come to a complete stop at the stop line (or before the crosswalk if no stop line), yield, then proceed; if you cannot see traffic from the first stop, move ahead slowly and stop again where you can see.
- [Ch VI] Yield sign: triangular, red and white; other traffic has the right-of-way; be ready to stop; stop only when traffic with the right-of-way requires it.
- [Ch VI] Speed limit signs: rectangular, black and white; show maximum and minimum limits (sometimes only at certain times, conditions, or for certain vehicles); never drive faster than the posted maximum or slower than the posted minimum.
- [Ch VI] Reduced Speed Ahead signs have the same color and shape as speed limit signs; begin slowing when you see one; the new limit begins at the next speed limit sign.
- [Ch VI] Lane use control signs: rectangular black and white; follow the movement required/permitted for your lane.
- [Ch VI] Do Not Pass signs: rectangular; do not pass where posted on two- or three-lane roads. No Passing Zone signs: pennant-shaped, yellow and black, placed on the left side of the road pointing to the start of the zone. Pass With Care signs: rectangular black and white, marking the end of a no-passing zone.
- [Ch VI] Exclusion signs: rectangular, with a red circle (or red circle and diagonal) on a white or black-and-white background; they restrict or prohibit vehicles, movements or activities.
- [Ch VI] Other regulatory signs are rectangular with black or red lettering on white.
- [Ch VI] Warning signs are usually diamond-shaped with a black legend and border on yellow; the manual illustrates: divided highway, divided highway ends, slippery when wet, pedestrian crossing, hill, lane ends / road narrows, school crossing or school zone, traffic signal ahead.
- [Ch VI] Guide and information signs include route markers, location and distance signs, park/recreation signs and service signs.
- [Ch VI] Work zones: orange is the basic color for construction/maintenance devices; most work-area signs are diamond-shaped; channelizing devices include barricades, vertical panels, drums, cones and tubes, often with flashing or steady lights at night.
- [Ch VI] Barricade/panel stripes slope downward toward the side traffic must pass: stripes sloping down to the right mean bear right; down to the left, bear left.
- [Ch VI] Flashing arrow panels in work zones guide drivers into lanes and warn that part of the road ahead is closed.
- [Ch VI] Flagpersons in work zones wear orange vests/shirts/jackets and use red flags or stop/slow paddles; obey them.

## Crashes and insurance (Chapters VII–VIII)

- [Ch VII] A crash involving any injury, death, or property damage of $3,000 or more must be reported to the police immediately by the quickest means of communication.
- [Ch VII] If you are in a crash: stop immediately and, if possible, move the vehicle off the travelled roadway; never leave without identifying yourself and rendering assistance.
- [Ch VII] Help the injured: call the police, ask for an ambulance if needed; do not move the injured unnecessarily; keep the injured warm; apply direct pressure to severe bleeding; protect victims from oncoming traffic.
- [Ch VII] Warn passing traffic (have someone warn approaching cars; carry a flashlight and flares).
- [Ch VII] Exchange information: give your name, address and vehicle registration information, and show your driver's license if requested, to anyone injured or to the driver/occupant/attendant of any damaged vehicle or property.
- [Ch VII] If you strike an unattended vehicle: stop, locate the owner, or leave a written notice with your name, address and the circumstances; if you damage other property along the highway, notify the owner.
- [Ch VIII] Safety Responsibility Law affects drivers in crashes causing death, injury or property damage over $3,000, and persons convicted of certain vehicle offenses.
- [Ch VIII] After such a crash: report to the police and your insurance company and file evidence of insurance (Form SR-21).
- [Ch VIII] After conviction of certain offenses: file and maintain proof of financial responsibility for 3 years from the conviction date (Form SR-22); failure to comply means license suspension.
- [Ch VIII] Every owner of a car, bus or truck must have motor vehicle insurance to register or operate the vehicle in Hawaii.
- [Ch VIII] Minimum coverage: personal injury protection benefits up to $10,000 per person (medical/rehabilitative); liability $20,000 per person / $40,000 per accident for bodily injury; $10,000 per accident for property damage.
- [Ch VIII] Bodily-injury lawsuits are allowed only when medical bills exceed $5,000, or there is loss of use of a body part/function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death; property-damage suits are allowed at any time.
- [Ch VIII] Insurance-law violations: fine up to $5,000, or up to 30 days imprisonment, or license suspension, or forfeiture of the registration certificate, or any combination, per violation.

## Alcohol and drugs (Chapter IX — Alcohol and Other Drugs)

- [Ch IX] Driving under the influence of intoxicants is prohibited in Hawaii.
- [Ch IX] BAC 0.08 or more = under the influence of alcohol; BAC more than 0.05 but less than 0.08 = impaired.
- [Ch IX] Zero tolerance: drivers under 21 may not operate a motor vehicle with a BAC of 0.02 or more.
- [Ch IX] Implied consent: anyone who operates a motor vehicle on Hawaii public highways is deemed to have consented to a test to determine the alcohol content of their blood.
- [Ch IX] Administrative revocation: if a test shows BAC 0.08 or more, or the driver refuses the test, the license is taken away and a 30-day temporary driving permit is issued; the driver may request a hearing.
- [Ch IX] Refusing a test requested by an officer may result in revocation for 1, 2 or 4 years depending on the driver's prior alcohol enforcement contacts; the driver may still be charged with DUI despite refusing; a test can also prove the driver was not under the influence.
- [Ch IX] It is against the law to consume or possess any alcoholic beverage on any public highway or public sidewalk; storing an opened alcoholic container in the driver/passenger area of a vehicle on a public road is prohibited.
- [Ch IX] Alcohol is a depressant, not a stimulant: it slows reflexes, interferes with judgment, reduces alertness and impairs vision; feeling stimulated is only lowered inhibitions (loss of caution and self-control).
- [Ch IX] The type of drink (beer, wine, whiskey) does not matter; what matters is the amount of alcohol that enters the blood.
- [Ch IX] Alcohol on an empty stomach affects the driver more rapidly than after a meal.
- [Ch IX] Once alcohol is in the blood, nothing lessens its effect: black coffee, food and cold showers may wake you but do not sober you; the liver burns alcohol off and the kidneys/lungs eliminate it — the process takes time.
- [Ch IX] Alcohol is a factor in about 40% (over 18,000) of US fatal crashes each year (manual's figure).
- [Ch IX] Prescription and over-the-counter medicines can impair driving: ask your doctor about driving effects before taking medicine; some allergy remedies and cold pills affect driving ability.
- [Ch IX] Amphetamines ("pep pills"/"speed") and tranquilizers/sedatives ("downers") greatly impair driving and can be fatal in overdose.
- [Ch IX] Alcohol combined with drugs can have a much more pronounced effect than either alone; never mix them.
- [Ch IX] Illegal drugs such as LSD, heroin and cocaine can make users unaware of or indifferent to their surroundings; anyone under their influence must not operate a motor vehicle; illegal-market drugs are dangerous because strength and purity are unknown.

## Safe driving techniques (Chapter X — Safe Driving Techniques)

- [Ch X] Bad driving habits fall into five causes: laziness, impatience, inattention, arrogance, ignorance.
- [Ch X] Distracted driving: avoid eating, drinking, phone use, reading, smoking, grooming and similar activities while driving; pull over in a safe place to read a map or take a call; even hands-free phone use diverts attention; pets should ride in a carrier, never in the driver's lap.
- [Ch X] Keep the radio volume low enough to hear sirens, horns and tire screech; do not drive with headsets or earphones covering or in both ears.
- [Ch X] Defensive driving: identify dangerous situations and act before a crash develops; stay alert and keep your eyes moving; look well ahead and anticipate chain reactions; always have an alternate plan (leave yourself a way out); keep proper following distance and proper speed for conditions.
- [Ch X] Scanning: devote most attention ahead, with regular glances to the sides, mirrors and instrument panel; speed reduces your field of vision.
- [Ch X] Do not drive when ill, injured, emotionally upset or under the influence of intoxicants; follow your physician's advice about medication and driving.
- [Ch X] Warning lamps: all instrument warning lamps should light when the ignition is turned on (a lamp that does not light is not functioning); after starting, a remaining yellow lamp is a warning and a red lamp means danger — check immediately; if the brake warning lamp lights when the pedal is pressed, the brake system is defective and the vehicle should not be driven.
- [Ch X] Seat belts must be worn at all times when driving or riding; they prevent ejection (your chance of being killed is five times greater if thrown from the car), prevent striking the interior (survival is possible up to 60 mph with lap-shoulder belts; death is possible as low as 12 mph unbelted), prevent injuring other occupants, and keep the driver at the controls.
- [Ch X] Lap belt: snug across the hip bones and lower abdomen; shoulder belt: loose enough to fit a fist between belt and chest.
- [Ch X] Never let a child ride in someone's lap or standing on a seat; no one can hold even a small infant through a crash or sudden stop; use child seats for children too small for regular belts.
- [Ch X] Hawaii law prohibits leaving a child under 9 years of age unattended in a motor vehicle for 5 minutes or longer.
- [Ch X] Backing: check the area behind before entering the vehicle; turn around and look to the rear — do not rely on mirrors; if you cannot see, have someone outside guide you; back slowly; avoid backing into traffic or across/into a roadway; watch the front of the vehicle when backing and turning; the driver leaving a parking space bears the chief responsibility for avoiding a collision.
- [Ch X] Entering traffic from a driveway: stop before crossing the sidewalk area (failing to stop is unlawful), move forward slowly watching for pedestrians and vehicles, then enter the nearest lane and accelerate to traffic speed.
- [Ch X] Roundabout: a one-way circular intersection where entering traffic yields to circulating traffic; each approach has a yield sign (and may have yield line markings). Slow down on approach; yield to pedestrians and bicyclists entering and exiting; look left and yield to traffic (including bicyclists) already in the roundabout; drive to the right of the central island, counterclockwise; keep speed low; signal right to exit; if you miss your exit, go around again.
- [Ch X] Lane choice: with two lanes each direction in free-flowing traffic use the right lane, passing on the left; with three or more lanes, the center lanes have fewer conflicts; slower traffic uses the right lane; avoid continual lane changing (lane hopping).
- [Ch X] Lane changing: check ahead, sides and rear using mirrors and a physical blind-spot check (turn your head); signal well in advance — the law requires signalling at least 100 feet before starting the change; move gradually and steadily; always yield to vehicles already in the other lane; never change lanes when approaching or moving through intersections and crosswalks.
- [Ch X] Following interval: count "one thousand one, one thousand two" from a fixed marker the vehicle ahead passes; you should never have less than a 2-second interval.
- [Ch X] Keep pace with traffic and a steady speed; reduce speed in poor road/weather conditions; never let emotions set your speed.
- [Ch X] Turns and curves: get into the proper lane well in advance; turn from and into the nearest lane; slow before entering a curve or turn and keep a steady speed through it (braking or abrupt speed changes in a turn can cause loss of control); do not swing wide or cut short; if you are not in the proper lane, continue and turn elsewhere.
- [Ch X] Waiting to turn left at a red light: when it turns green, move slowly into the intersection with wheels pointing straight ahead and turn when clear and safe.
- [Ch X] Always stop completely before turning right on a red signal; when turning right at an intersection, do not drive in a bike lane; watch for pedestrians, bicyclists and motorcyclists when turning.
- [Ch X] Passing: pass only a vehicle moving below the safe speed; pass on the left except for vehicles making/about to make a left turn (pass those carefully on the right); never drive onto the shoulder to pass a left-turning vehicle; check ahead, signal, physically check behind, sound the horn, accelerate, and move back only when you can see the passed vehicle in your interior rear-view mirror; if a conflict appears mid-pass, brake sharply and return behind the vehicle rather than speeding up; never pass when approaching or moving through an intersection or crosswalk; when passing on the right on multi-lane roads, remember you approach in the other driver's blind spot.
- [Ch X] Passing parked cars: watch for movement inside, lit stop/back-up/signal lamps, exhaust vapor, and turning front wheels; assume a just-stopped driver will open the door as you approach.
- [Ch X] Never drive into an intersection or crosswalk unless there is space for your entire vehicle on the far side; do not stop within an intersection or crosswalk; stops to load/unload or talk must be made out of a traffic lane.
- [Ch X] Braking: hard braking can lock/slide wheels and remove steering control — instead apply and release rapidly and steer toward a clear area (for ABS, follow the owner's manual); avoid braking while turning; use brakes cautiously on water, ice, snow, frost, sand or gravel; test brakes after driving through water and dry them with light repeated applications; when stopping in traffic, stop far enough behind to see where the rear tires of the vehicle ahead touch the road; avoid prolonged braking (overheating) — shift to a lower gear on downgrades.
- [Ch X] Downshifting gives engine braking on steep grades and should be done before starting the downgrade; routine downshifting to slow down is not recommended (no stop-lamp warning to following drivers) — use it only to supplement the brakes; it may be used in an emergency for partial/complete brake failure.
- [Ch X] Hill parking (Hawaii manual rule): always set the hand brake; shift to PARK (or reverse/low gear); always turn the front wheels toward the curb — whether parking uphill or downhill, turn the wheels to the right, so the vehicle would roll away from traffic if it rolled free.
- [Ch X] Parallel parking: curbside wheels within 12 inches of the curb; front and rear bumpers no closer than 2 feet from other vehicles (unless the street is marked otherwise); where angle parking is not designated, park parallel to the curb heading in the direction of traffic.
- [Ch X] Electric turn/stop/hazard signals have been standard since 1968; hand signals are the backup when electric signals fail and drivers must know them.
- [Ch X] Rain: the first few drops are the danger point — oil not yet washed away makes the road as slippery as ice; reduce speed and allow at least twice the normal following distance.
- [Ch X] Hydroplaning: in heavy/prolonged rain a water layer forms; good-tread tires wipe the surface up to about 35 mph; partial hydroplaning may start at 35 mph; at 55 mph in heavy rain tires may lose all contact with the pavement (no braking, accelerating or turning possible). Prevention: good tread depth, proper inflation (worn/bald or underinflated tires hydroplane at lower speeds), and reduced speed.
- [Ch X] Wet brakes after deep water/heavy rain may pull to one side or not hold; apply gentle pressure until they work properly again.
- [Ch X] In heavy rain: reduce speed and use low-beam head lamps so others can see you; if you cannot see clearly, pull onto the shoulder or a clear area and stop until it eases.
- [Ch X] Never drive through a flooded area where swiftly moving water is over 1 foot deep — it can sweep the vehicle off the road.
- [Ch X] Fog (mainly at higher elevations in Hawaii): sharply reduce speed; use low beams only — high beams reflect and reduce your own and oncoming drivers' vision; be ready for an emergency stop; if you cannot see at least two car lengths (40 feet), pull completely off the pavement, stop, and turn on hazard signals.
- [Ch X] Dawn/dusk: use visors/sunglasses against sun glare; keep glass clean; in the low-light period before sunrise and after sunset, turn head lamps on low beam so others can see you; watch for unlighted objects and pedestrians.
- [Ch X] Night driving: reduce speed so you can stop within the distance you can see; do not use high beams when approaching or following other vehicles; if an oncoming vehicle's lights are too bright, flash your high beams once and look at the right edge of the road, using lane markings/reflectors as a guide; red lane-marking reflectors mean you are going the wrong direction in the lane.
- [Ch X] Freeways have no stop signs, signals, railroad crossings, steep hills or sharp curves, and entrances/exits only at planned locations.
- [Ch X] Entering a freeway: if the entrance lane is congested, slow or stop near the entrance — do not crawl to the end of the entrance lane and stop there; when clear, signal, accelerate to match freeway speed, and merge (slip in behind an oncoming vehicle if the near lane is occupied).
- [Ch X] On the freeway: through traffic uses left/center lanes at or near the limit; the right lane is for slower traffic and should stay open for entering/exiting vehicles; avoid lane changes; never stop unless absolutely unavoidable; never cross the center divider, turn around, or back up — if you need the opposite direction, go to the next exit and re-enter.
- [Ch X] Freeway emergency stop: try to reach the next exit; if you must stop, drive entirely off the roadway as far from traffic as possible; never stand or walk on the freeway; do not stop to help other drivers — report it to the authorities.
- [Ch X] Leaving the freeway: signal, move into the exit lane, keep your speed until fully on the exit ramp, then slow smoothly; if you miss your exit, continue to the next one — never stop or back up.
- [Ch X] Emergencies generally: do not panic; avoid slamming the brakes and sliding the wheels; steer toward a less dangerous location; keep trying to control the vehicle even after striking an object; wear belts so you stay in position to control the vehicle.
- [Ch X] Tire blowout: do NOT apply the brakes; grip the wheel firmly and stay in your lane; slow gradually without braking; when moving very slowly, brake lightly and pull off into a safe area; check tire pressure and condition monthly.
- [Ch X] Loss of a wheel: use the same procedure as a blowout; partial/total brake failure is likely; once stopped the vehicle cannot be moved and must be towed.
- [Ch X] Complete brake failure: pump the pedal quickly; shift to a lower gear; look for an escape route (side road or flat open area) and warn others with lights and horn; apply the parking brake gradually (a rapid, hard application can cause loss of control).
- [Ch X] Steering failure: if power steering is partially lost, use greater effort and do not give up; if steering fails completely, slow gradually and brake as needed to stop smoothly.
- [Ch X] Wheels off the pavement: do not jerk the wheel to force the vehicle back; keep driving with wheels off the pavement while reducing speed; brake gently; at a safe speed, steer back onto the pavement and be ready to correct to stay in your lane.
- [Ch X] Helping a passing vehicle in danger: steer as far right as safely possible (even onto the shoulder); if it keeps trying to pass, slow so it can move in ahead of you; if it is dropping back to return behind you, accelerate to make room — only after being certain of the other driver's intention.
- [Ch X] Oncoming vehicle in your lane: flash lights and honk; slow quickly and steer right into any clear area, even off the road; never enter the oncoming traffic lane; avoid solid objects and pedestrians.
- [Ch X] Stuck accelerator: slap the pedal hard (fully depress and quickly release); turn the ignition off (only far enough to stop the engine, not to engage the steering lock); apply the brakes; pull off into a safe area; power steering/brakes will need more effort with the engine off.
- [Ch X] Power steering failure: more effort is needed; reduce speed, drive to a safe area and stop.
- [Ch X] Skids are mostly caused by sudden changes of direction or speed, above all excessive speed for conditions. To recover: avoid the brakes (release them if braking started the skid); ease off the accelerator; steer in the direction the rear of the vehicle is skidding (right for a right skid, left for a left skid), firmly but without a large sudden turn; as the skid stops, straighten the wheel; overcorrecting can skid the vehicle the opposite way — steer gently into the new skid and repeat until controlled.
- [Ch X] Vehicle fire: stop in a safe area clear of traffic; turn off the ignition and all electrical switches; get all occupants out and away, off the roadway; small fires may be smothered with an extinguisher, dirt, sand or a blanket — never use water on gasoline, oil or electrical fires; do not attempt a large gasoline/oil fire — seek assistance.
- [Ch X] Breakdown/engine failure: move the vehicle to a safe location if possible; keep occupants away from traffic (if the vehicle cannot be moved but a safe place is reachable, move occupants there; otherwise occupants stay in the vehicle); use hazard lights, flares, reflectors, flashlight; signal for help by raising the hood and tying a white cloth to the antenna or door handle; be cautious of strangers offering help — stay locked in until sure of intentions, or ask them to notify police; if you must walk along the highway, walk facing traffic, well clear of the roadway; never walk on a freeway — stay with the vehicle.

## Large vehicles, trailers, motorcycles (Chapter XI — Trucks, Trailers, Buses and Motorcycles)

- [Ch XI] Large vehicles: the area immediately behind the vehicle is invisible to its driver (side/rear visibility only by mirrors); they need more space and caution for turning, lane changes and backing; other road users should stay where the large-vehicle driver can see them and give extra space.
- [Ch XI] Large vehicles need greater stopping distances and accelerate slowly; shift to a lower gear before descending a steep hill because continuous braking overheats brakes and makes them ineffective; other drivers should move out of the way of an overtaking large vehicle on a downgrade.
- [Ch XI] Slipstream: fast-moving large vehicles create wind gusts strong enough to change the direction of smaller vehicles; be ready to correct your steering when meeting or passing them.
- [Ch XI] Hazardous materials: drivers must ensure dangerous loads are properly packaged and marked, and the vehicle marked with the type of material carried.
- [Ch XI] Large vehicles must carry emergency equipment such as fire extinguishers, flares, emergency reflectors and first aid kits.
- [Ch XI] Towing: if a trailer blocks the view through the inside rear-view mirror, outside mirrors of proper size are required on both sides.
- [Ch XI] A loaded trailer weighing more than half the towing vehicle's weight, or 3,000 lb or more, must have brakes and breakaway protection.
- [Ch XI] Passengers must not ride in trailers; riding in house trailers is prohibited.
- [Ch XI] Safety chains must be attached to the frames of the towing vehicle and the trailer — not to any part of the trailer hitch; do not rely on bumper hitches.
- [Ch XI] Trailer load: about 10–15% of the trailer's weight should rest on the hitch; too much weight makes steering difficult; too little makes the trailer "fishtail"; do not overload; secure the load.
- [Ch XI] Motorcycles are hard to judge: their narrow, short profile can make them look farther away and slower than they are — take a second and third look; over two-thirds of car/motorcycle crashes result from a car driver turning in front of the motorcyclist.
- [Ch XI] A motorcycle is entitled to the full width of a lane; never share a lane with a motorcyclist; riders change lane position (left, center, right) to see and be seen and to avoid hazards such as potholes or railroad tracks, sometimes suddenly.
- [Ch XI] Intersections are the most likely place for car/motorcycle collisions; motorcycle turn signals do not self-cancel, so a rider may forget to cancel one — be sure of the rider's intention before moving into their path.
- [Ch XI] Following a motorcycle: allow at least a 2-second following distance, and increase the space because a motorcycle's speed and distance are hard to judge.
- [Ch XI] Passing a motorcycle: give it a full lane; never crowd into its lane; returning to your lane too soon can force the rider to swerve right into traffic or off the road; if a motorcycle passes you, maintain your lane position and speed.
- [Ch XI] Motorcycles must be registered and insured to the same standards as an automobile, and riders need a separate motorcycle operator license (knowledge + skills tests).

## Pedestrians and school buses (Chapter XII — Pedestrian Safety)

- [Ch XII] In Hawaii, 20% of all traffic fatalities are pedestrians; almost half of those killed are under 15 or over 65; almost 90% of fatal pedestrian crashes occur in the roadway, over half while crossing it.
- [Ch XII] As a driver: slow down and be prepared to stop whenever people are walking on or crossing the roadway and whenever children are near the roadway; sound the horn when necessary to warn a pedestrian (required by law).
- [Ch XII] Stop for all blind persons in the roadway (identified by a white cane or guide dog) regardless of the traffic signal or situation.
- [Ch XII] Never drive through a pedestrian safety zone; never pass a vehicle that is stopped to let a pedestrian cross the street (both prohibited by law).
- [Ch XII] Be especially watchful for children near schools, parks, playgrounds and residential areas; stop at the signal of any school crossing guard; watch for pedestrians when turning at intersections and when entering or leaving driveways.
- [Ch XII] Before opening your door or letting passengers out, look for approaching vehicles; exit on the side away from traffic whenever possible.
- [Ch XII] As a pedestrian: cross only at crosswalks (jaywalking is illegal); obey traffic laws and signals; never enter the street or crosswalk when vehicles are approaching close by; never start across on a green light that has been showing for some time — wait for the next cycle; look for turning vehicles; never run alongside or across a street; walk on the left side of the street facing oncoming traffic; wear bright clothing; do not stand on the travelled roadway to solicit or to guard a vehicle/parking place; children must not play in the street.
- [Ch XII] School bus: when a school bus is stopped on a highway with alternating red lamps flashing, drivers in the bus's lane and all lanes adjacent to it must stop before reaching the bus and stay stopped until the bus moves again or the red lamps are turned off; violation can cost $1,000 (§291C-95, HRS).
- [Ch XII] Vehicles travelling in the opposite direction on a divided highway (two roadways separated by a strip of land or other space not intended for vehicular travel) are not required to stop for a school bus.

## Bicycles (Chapter XIII — Bicycle Safety)

- [Ch XIII] Bicycles are legally vehicles, with the same rights and responsibilities as other vehicles; bicyclists who violate traffic laws face the same penalties as drivers.
- [Ch XIII] Passing a bicyclist: a motorist should keep at least 3 feet of space between the vehicle and the bicyclist until safely past; do not pass a bicyclist and then immediately slow and turn right without leaving enough space.
- [Ch XIII] When making a right turn, stay clear of bike lanes and look for and yield to bicyclists when crossing a bike lane; if you plan an immediate right turn, it is better not to pass the bicyclist first.
- [Ch XIII] Opening a car door so as to create a hazard for moving traffic, including bicyclists, is a traffic violation — look before opening your door.
- [Ch XIII] Bicyclists must ride with traffic (riding against traffic causes about 25% of bicycle-car crashes); when travelling slower than traffic, ride as close to the right edge as practicable; on one-way roads, riding on the left edge is legal.
- [Ch XIII] Bicyclists may move into the travel lane to make a left turn, to avoid hazards, when moving at traffic speed, or when the lane is too narrow to share safely; on the roadway they must ride single file (two abreast only on a wide-enough bike lane/path that does not impede traffic).
- [Ch XIII] Bicyclists must obey all traffic signs and signals and follow lane markings, and move out of a right-turn-only lane if going straight.
- [Ch XIII] Riding at night requires a headlight and a rear reflector (a rear light and pedal reflectors, and light-colored clothing, are recommended).
- [Ch XIII] Do not carry passengers on a bicycle unless it is equipped to do so.
- [Ch XIII] Bicycles are not allowed on sidewalks in business districts; elsewhere sidewalk riding is allowed at 10 mph or less, always yielding the right-of-way to pedestrians.
- [Ch XIII] Bicycle safety: wear a helmet (recommended though not legally required); be predictable; do not wear headphones while riding; keep at least 3 feet from parked cars against opening doors; a left turn may be made like a motor vehicle, or by riding to the crosswalk and walking the bicycle across.

## Definitions (manual front matter)

- [Definitions] Abandoned vehicle: a vehicle left unattended on the highway for more than 24 hours.
- [Definitions] Bus: a motor vehicle designed to carry more than 10 passengers and used to transport persons.
- [Definitions] Bicycle (definition): a device with two or three wheels of 16 inches or greater diameter, propelled by human power.
- [Definitions] Crosswalk: the part of a roadway at an intersection within the extensions of the sidewalk lines, or any part of a roadway distinctly marked for pedestrian crossing.
- [Definitions] Safety zone: an area within a roadway set apart for the exclusive use of pedestrians, protected or plainly marked.
- [Definitions] GVWR: the weight of the vehicle plus its load-carrying capacity.
- [Definitions] Emergency vehicle: police/fire (or similar federal) vehicles and ambulances, and designated private vehicles, equipped with a flashing blue or red light and a warning sound system.
