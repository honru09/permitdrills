# Fact Sheet — Maryland (facts only; one fact per line; tagged by manual section)

Source: Maryland Driver's Manual, DL-002, October 2026 Edition (English PDF, https://mva.maryland.gov/media/148), cross-checked against the Traditional-Chinese DL-002 PDF (https://mva.maryland.gov/media/134). No manual sentences are reproduced here — facts, numbers, and procedures only.

## Section 1: Driving Tests Requirements
- [S1.A] Vision standard for a license: binocular vision; visual acuity at least 20/40 in each eye; continuous field of vision at least 140 degrees.
- [S1.A] If glasses/contacts are needed to meet the vision standard, they must always be worn while driving; the license shows this restriction.
- [S1.B] Knowledge test: 25 questions; 20 minutes allowed; taken at an MVA location; covers motor vehicle laws, safe driving practices, traffic signs.
- [S1.C] Driving skills test: appointment required; taken on public roads plus a course at the test site; only the applicant and the examiner (plus an MVA-approved interpreter if needed) may be in the vehicle.
- [S1.C] Skills-test vehicle must have current plates and registration, proof of insurance, be safe to drive, have no windshield cracks/obstructions affecting vision, and at least a half tank of gas.
- [S1.C] A supervising driver must accompany the applicant to the skills test site.
- [S1.D] Medical conditions that could affect driving ability must be reported to the MVA (state law).

## Section 2: Licensing Requirements and Process
- [S2 intro] A noncommercial license is also called a Class C license; it covers cars, pickup trucks, and vans.
- [S2 intro] Application requires: proof of age/identity, proof of lawful status if born outside the US, Social Security Number or proof of ineligibility, and two proofs of Maryland residency; originals or certified copies only (no photocopies/notarized copies).
- [S2.A] New drivers go through the Rookie Driver / Graduated Licensing System (GLS): Learner's Instructional Permit → Provisional License → Driver's License. GLS applies to first-time licensees of all ages.
- [S2.B] Type 1 Learner's Instructional Permit: for people who have never held a license; valid up to 2 years; requires vision screening + knowledge test.
- [S2.B] Type 2 Learner's Instructional Permit: for someone who already holds a license and wants a different class; minimum age 16 years 6 months; valid up to 180 days; driving test may be taken after holding it at least 14 days.
- [S2.B] Applicants under 25: minimum age for a learner's permit is 15 years 9 months.
- [S2.B] Applicants under 18: parent/legal guardian must co-sign the application.
- [S2.B] Applicants under 16: must provide a completed Learner's Permit School Attendance Certification form (DL-300).
- [S2.B] Permit holders under 25 must: complete the Maryland Driver Education Program; log at least 60 practice hours with a supervising driver, at least 10 of them in the window from 30 minutes before sunset to 30 minutes after sunrise; keep a Practice and Skills Log.
- [S2.B] Holding periods before Provisional eligibility (under 25): under 18 — at least 9 months violation-free; age 18 with diploma/GED or ages 19–24 — at least 3 months violation-free.
- [S2.B] Applicants 25 and older: must complete driver education; log at least 14 practice hours, at least 3 of them in the sunset/sunrise window; keep a Practice and Skills Log; hold the permit at least 45 days before Provisional eligibility.
- [S2.B] Any applicant convicted of (or granted Probation Before Judgment for) a moving violation, or suspended for any reason, must hold the permit at least 9 months after the most recent conviction/PBJ date before Provisional eligibility.
- [S2.C] Provisional License: allows unsupervised driving with restrictions; must be held at least 18 months before eligibility for a full Driver's License.
- [S2.C] Provisional requirements: completed approved driver education; valid unexpired learner's permit; required violation-free holding period met; no moving-violation convictions/PBJ during that period; signed Practice and Skills Log showing 60 practice hours (14 if age 25+); pass the driving skills test.
- [S2.D] Full Driver's License eligibility: at least 18 years old; Provisional License held at least 18 months; no moving-violation convictions/PBJ within the past 18 months; MVA converts the Provisional License automatically and mails a card to carry with it.
- [S2.E] Co-signer for a minor (under 18): parent/legal guardian; proof = original/certified birth certificate with parent's name or court guardianship documents; a spouse 18+ may co-sign with proof of marriage; otherwise a responsible adult (e.g., an employer) may co-sign with proof such as parents' death certificates or proof of emancipation.
- [S2.F] A co-signer may cancel a minor's license by written request to the MVA Administrative Adjudication Division; the license stays canceled until age 18 unless a co-signer signs a new application.
- [S2.G] Out-of-country license holders must pass vision screening, knowledge test, driving skills test, and a 3-hour MVA-approved roadway safety driving education program; a non-English license also requires an International License or an English translation by an MVA-approved provider/court translator or the embassy of the country of origin, with translator's typed name, phone number, and signature.

## Section 3: Basic Driving
- [S3.A] Defensive driving: constantly observe front, sides, and rear; search ahead; be ready to change speed or lanes; never assume other drivers will do what they should.
- [S3.B] Yield means letting other vehicles, bicyclists, or pedestrians go first; never assume you automatically have the right-of-way.
- [S3.B] Yield to: the driver who arrived first at an intersection; the driver on your right when arriving at the same time (including at a four-way stop); pedestrians, bicyclists, and drivers still in the intersection; drivers already on the road at a T-intersection; others when a yield sign faces you; opposing traffic when you turn left; drivers on a road you enter from a driveway/private road; drivers already on a highway when you are on an entrance/acceleration ramp.
- [S3.C] Posted speed limit = maximum legal speed under ideal conditions; driving faster than the posted limit is illegal; speed must be reasonable for conditions and hazards.
- [S3.C] Reduce speed for: sharp curves/hills with limited sight, slippery roads, areas with pedestrians or animals, shopping centers/parking lots/downtown areas, heavy traffic, narrow bridges and tunnels, toll plazas, schools/playgrounds/residential streets, railroad grade crossings.
- [S3.C] Speeding effects (facts stated): reduces vehicle control and curve handling, lengthens stopping distance, reduces hazard recognition/reaction, makes distance harder for others to judge, increases crash force.
- [S3.D] Recommended minimum following distance: 3 to 4 seconds under ideal conditions, measured against a fixed reference point.
- [S3.D] Behind vehicles that stop often (buses, delivery vans): increase following distance to 4 or 5 seconds, more if needed.
- [S3.E] Stopping distance depends on: perception/reaction time, road type and condition, tire tread condition, brake condition.
- [S3.F] Drive in a single lane; do not drift across lane lines or change lanes inside an intersection; keep right of the center of the road except when passing a same-direction vehicle, when a sign/signal allows, or when an obstruction makes it necessary.
- [S3.G] Turning: check signs/signals, plan ahead, signal before the turn, check front/behind/both sides, adjust speed.
- [S3.H] U-turn: check legality first; signal left, stop, yield to oncoming traffic; when clear, turn into the outside/right-hand lane traveling the opposite direction.
- [S3.I] Passing: estimate time/space first; signal before passing; leave plenty of space; return only after seeing both headlights of the passed vehicle in the rear-view mirror.
- [S3.I] Passing is generally on the left; passing on the right is allowed only if safe, staying on the road, and: the other vehicle is about to turn left, OR the road is unobstructed with 2+ same-direction lanes, OR it is an unobstructed one-way road with 2+ lanes.
- [S3.I] When being passed: yield to the passing vehicle; do not increase speed.
- [S3.I] Passing is prohibited: in a no-passing zone; where a solid yellow line is on your side; where there are double solid yellow lines; when it would interfere with safe operation of other vehicles; approaching a hilltop or curve without a clear view ahead; when view is obstructed within 100 feet of a bridge, elevated road, or tunnel; when crossing or within 100 feet of an intersection or railroad grade crossing; on the shoulder, right or left.
- [S3.J] Parking: park only in areas meant for parking; by a road, park as far from traffic as possible and close to the curb; set the parking brake; gear (manual) or Park (automatic); check mirrors/traffic before opening the door; take keys and lock the vehicle.
- [S3.J] Where "stay right" signs are posted, use the right-hand lane(s) unless passing.

## Section 4: Signs, Signals and Pavement Markings
- [S4 intro] Traffic control devices = signals, signs, pavement markings; everyone must obey them; the only exceptions are directions from a police officer/emergency personnel or a uniformed school crossing guard; driving across private property or leaving the road to avoid a device is not allowed.
- [S4.A] Steady red signal: complete stop at the stop line; if none, before the crosswalk; if neither, before entering the intersection; remain stopped while red.
- [S4.A] Turn on red: unless a sign prohibits it, after a complete stop you may turn right, or turn left from a one-way street onto another one-way street; when turning on red, yield to pedestrians and all other traffic.
- [S4.A] Steady yellow signal: the signal is changing green to red; stop safely if able; if too close to stop safely, proceed carefully through; vehicles already in the intersection get time to clear.
- [S4.A] Steady green signal: proceed with caution after checking the intersection is clear; yield to pedestrians and vehicles already in the intersection; a sign/signal may still prohibit a turn.
- [S4.A] Steady red arrow: complete stop before the stop line/crosswalk/intersection if traveling in the arrow's direction; remain stopped while the arrow is red.
- [S4.A] Steady yellow arrow: permission to turn in the arrow's direction is ending; slow down, proceed with caution.
- [S4.A] Steady green arrow: proceed with caution in the arrow's direction; yield to pedestrians and vehicles already in the intersection.
- [S4.A] Flashing red signal: complete stop at the stop line (or before crosswalk/intersection), yield to all traffic and pedestrians, proceed when clear; at a railroad crossing, stop even if no train is seen, and proceed only when the signal stops and the way is clear.
- [S4.A] Flashing yellow signal: slow down and proceed with caution.
- [S4.A] Flashing red arrow: complete stop before the stop line/crosswalk/intersection; yield to all traffic and pedestrians; proceed in the arrow's direction when clear.
- [S4.A] Lane-use (reversible lane) signals: never drive in a lane under a red X; a green arrow allows driving in the lane; a steady yellow X means move out of the lane as soon as safely possible; a flashing yellow X (or two-way left-turn arrows) means the lane may be used for a left turn, shared with opposing left-turning traffic.
- [S4.A] Signal not working: stop at the stop line; if none, before the crosswalk; if neither, before the intersection; yield to vehicles and pedestrians in the intersection; remain stopped until safe to proceed.
- [S4.B] Sign colors: red = stop/yield/do not enter/wrong way; yellow = general warning; white = speed limit, keep right, some guide signs; orange = construction/maintenance work-area warning; green = destinations, distances, directions; blue = services (food, gas, rest areas); brown = recreation and cultural interest; fluorescent yellow-green = school, pedestrian, bicycle signs; fluorescent pink = emergency traffic incidents; black = one-way signs and changeable-message signs.
- [S4.B] Sign shapes: octagon (8 sides) = stop, always a complete stop; inverted triangle = yield (the only sign with this shape); diamond = warning; pennant = no-passing zone begins, do not pass; vertical rectangle = usually instructions/road rules (regulatory); horizontal rectangle = usually directions/information; pentagon (5 sides) = school zone / school crossing; round = railroad crossing ahead (advance warning); crossbuck (X shape) = identifies the location of a railroad crossing; trapezoid = recreation/cultural interest areas and National Forest routes.
- [S4.B] Stop sign procedure: complete stop at the stop line; if none, before the crosswalk; if neither, before the intersection; yield to vehicles and pedestrians in the intersection; stay stopped until safe to proceed.
- [S4.B] Yield sign procedure: slow down, look left and right, yield to pedestrians, bicycles, and vehicles; proceed only when safe.
- [S4.B] Speed limit sign (black on white, rectangular): states the maximum speed limit for that part of the road.
- [S4.B] Other regulatory signs in the manual's chart include: No Right Turn, No Left Turn, No U-Turn, No Turns, Left Turn Only, Do Not Enter, No Parking, No Turn on Red, Do Not Pass, Stay to the Right of Median, Left Turn Yield on Green, Bike Lane, Wrong Way, Reserved Parking for people with disability permits, Two-Way Left Turn (center lane usable by both directions for left turns), One Way (traffic only in the arrow's direction), HOV lane (usable only with the posted number of occupants).
- [S4.B] Warning signs in the manual's chart include: Stop Ahead, Yield Ahead, Signal Ahead, Sharp Right Turn, Pedestrian Crossing, Traffic Circle/Roundabout, Advisory Speed (with exit ramp), No Passing Zone, Two Roads Merging, Road Divides Ahead, Divided Road Ends Ahead, Road Curves, Sharp Turns, Side Road Enters Ahead, Lane Ends / Merge Left, T Intersection Ahead, Railroad before T Intersection, Steep Hill, Slippery When Wet, Truck Crossing, Trail Crossing, Pedestrian and Bicycle Crossing, Deer or Other Wildlife Crossing, Flagger Ahead, Roadwork Ahead, Detour, School Crossing Ahead, School Bus Stop Ahead, Begin Work Zone, End Active Work Zone, Work Zone Active When Flashing (increased fines).
- [S4.B] Route markers identify numbered roads (Interstate, US Route, State Route); mile markers on interstates are placed every mile and give location information for help (tow truck, emergency personnel); service/guide signs identify services at exits (lodging, hospital, gas, food, airport, train station, park and ride).
- [S4.C] Broken white line: separates same-direction lanes; lane crossing permitted.
- [S4.C] Broken yellow line: separates opposite-direction traffic; careful passing permitted in either direction; used as the center line on two-lane two-way roads.
- [S4.C] Single solid white line: marks the right edge of the road; also separates same-direction lanes where lane changing is discouraged.
- [S4.C] Single solid yellow line: marks left edge lines on divided highways, one-way roads, and ramps.
- [S4.C] Double solid white lines: separate same-direction lanes; lane changes not allowed; may separate general lanes from HOV lanes; may appear before obstructions passable on either side.
- [S4.C] Double solid yellow lines: separate opposite-direction traffic; passing not allowed in either direction; a left turn across double solid yellow lines is allowed (e.g., into a driveway).
- [S4.C] Solid + broken yellow lines: passing permitted in one direction only — allowed from the broken-line side, not allowed from the solid-line side; also used at the edges of two-way left-turn lanes (solid outside, broken inside).
- [S4.C] Double broken yellow lines: mark edges of reversible lanes; entry/exit depends on signs and signals.
- [S4.C] Dotted yellow or white line: continues lane lines through intersections; stay within the lane markings in intersections.
- [S4.C] Stop line: white solid line showing where to stop for a stop sign or red signal.
- [S4.C] Yield lines: rows of small triangles across a lane showing where a vehicle must yield to other vehicles or pedestrians.
- [S4.C] Word/symbol markings: show lane use, lane reductions, travel direction, lane arrows, and "SCHOOL" / "RxR" warnings for school areas and railroad crossings.
- [S4.C] Crosswalk lines: white solid lines showing where pedestrians cross; extra lines may appear between or instead of them.
- [S4.C] Bike lanes: separated from motor-vehicle lanes by solid or broken white lines; may be marked with signs or bicycle symbols and arrows; shared-use lane markings alert drivers that bicyclists may be on the road, show bicyclists where to ride, and discourage wrong-direction biking.

## Section 5: Driving Situations and Conditions
- [S5.A] Reduced visibility: use the right edge lines/road edge as a guide; yellow markings are never on your right (yellow divides opposite-direction traffic and marks the left edge); white markings mark the right edge and divide same-direction traffic.
- [S5.A] Headlights must be on whenever there is not enough light to clearly see at least 1,000 feet ahead.
- [S5.A] Maryland law requires headlights on when windshield wipers are in use in inclement weather.
- [S5.A] Use low beams: in normal night driving on lit roads; in fog, rain, snow; when entering a tunnel or construction area.
- [S5.A] Use high beams on roads without streetlights; switch to low beams at least 500 feet before reaching oncoming vehicles; switch to low beams when following a vehicle 300 feet or less ahead.
- [S5.B] Night driving: do not stare into oncoming headlights; look at the road ahead or slightly right (right edge of lane/road); increase following distance.
- [S5.C] Fog: reduce speed; reduce further when seeing headlights/taillights ahead and be ready to stop quickly; use low beams only, plus fog lights if equipped; if fog is too thick to drive safely, pull completely off the road to a safe place and turn on emergency flashers.
- [S5.D] Wet/inclement weather: traction is the tire-road grip that allows starting, stopping, and direction changes; keep much greater following distance on wet roads, even more on snow/ice; reduce speed; avoid slamming brakes (skid risk); without ABS, pump the brakes to prevent skidding; avoid jerking the steering wheel or quick speed changes; keep wipers working; use headlights.
- [S5.D] Snow/ice: no speed is completely safe; black ice = thin, hard-to-see ice on asphalt from melting and re-freezing; four-wheel drive can also slide; slow down; clear all ice/snow from the vehicle before driving; start slowly and test brakes gently; begin slowing long before intersections/turns; keep gas tank and washer fluid full; use chains/snow tires/radials on designated snow emergency routes when a snow emergency is declared; bridges and ramps freeze before roads; plowed roads may refreeze at night or keep icy patches.
- [S5.D] Emergency kit items listed: flares, flashlight and batteries, first aid kit, blanket, kitty litter or sand, small shovel and ice scraper.
- [S5.E] Skid recovery: release the accelerator or brake pedal being used; steer in the direction of the skid; look where you want to go; brake only after traction returns; gentle acceleration may help if the rear wheels lose traction.
- [S5.F] Hydroplaning: at higher speed on water, tires can ride on a sheet of water and lose road contact; prevention = good tires + lower speed on wet roads; recovery = foot off the gas, do not brake, keep the vehicle straight, slow down until control returns.
- [S5.G] Railroad crossings: trains cannot stop quickly; buses, school buses, and trucks carrying hazardous materials must stop before every railroad crossing; if gates are down or warning lights flashing, the road is temporarily closed — stop and wait until gates rise and lights stop flashing; after a train passes, look both ways — another train may come on another track; never start across unless the vehicle can clear completely, with room on the far side; in an emergency on/near a crossing, exit the vehicle, move away from the tracks, and report using the blue-and-white Emergency Notification System (ENS) sign.
- [S5.H] Work zone: orange = work zone color; expect lane closures, detours, shifts, narrow lanes, drop-offs, reduced sight distance; obey posted limits (speeding fines may be much higher in work zones, higher still if workers are present); flashing amber/yellow lights = active work zone / workers present; flashing blue lights = active speed cameras; flashing green lights = highway maintenance vehicles; obey the flagger; minimize distractions; do not tailgate or change lanes unnecessarily.
- [S5.I] Traffic circles (roundabouts): approach roads have yield signs; always yield to traffic already in the circle; reduce speed; keep right of the island; yield to pedestrians in the crosswalk; wait at the yield line for a gap (no need to wait if the circle is empty); in a multi-lane circle, stay in your lane; signal right and move to the outside lane after passing the exit before yours.
- [S5.J] Limited-access highways/interstates: no intersections or traffic lights; entry via entrance ramp + acceleration lane (match the speed of traffic already on the highway); do not cross the solid lines dividing the entrance from the highway; if driving slower than traffic, use the right lane whenever possible; exit via the right lane well in advance, then the deceleration lane, slowing to the ramp's posted limit; if you take the wrong exit, continue off the ramp and find a way to re-enter — never stop and back up on any part of the highway.
- [S5.J] Stopping on an interstate/highway: stopping in moving-traffic lanes is not allowed; shoulder stopping only for a disabled vehicle or emergency; turn on emergency flashers and stay inside the vehicle if possible; standing/walking along an interstate is very dangerous.
- [S5.K] Funeral processions: participants turn on headlights and hazard flashers; a participant with lights on may proceed through a red signal; other drivers must yield to the procession — even on a green signal — until all procession vehicles have passed, unless they can proceed safely without crossing the procession's path.
- [S5.L] Slow-moving vehicles: include bicycles, horse-drawn vehicles, farm vehicles; the slow-moving vehicle sign on the rear warns the vehicle travels 25 mph or less; pass only when safe; if following while waiting to pass, use emergency flashers to warn traffic behind.

## Section 6: Dangerous Driving Behaviors
- [S6.A] BAC = blood alcohol concentration, the amount of alcohol in the blood.
- [S6.A] Maryland treats a driver as driving under the influence at BAC 0.08 or higher.
- [S6.A] Any amount of alcohol can impair driving; judgment and coordination can be impaired at a much lower BAC and can still bring criminal charges; effects can begin with one drink (vision, simple movements, reaction time).
- [S6.A] BAC cannot be judged by drink count alone; factors include weight, body fat, how long ago and how much the person ate, tiredness, mood, and medications.
- [S6.A] Under 21: drinking any alcohol is illegal; a driver under 21 stopped after drinking faces license suspension or revocation and a charge for violating the alcohol restriction on the license. (The manual states no numeric under-21 BAC figure.)
- [S6.A] Drunk/drugged driving conviction with a child in the vehicle: the judge can double the fine and jail time.
- [S6.A] Open container: driving with an open alcohol container in the passenger area is illegal; an open container includes any open can/bottle/container/package, and a multi-pack with open, empty, or missing bottles/cans can count; the passenger area = driver/passenger seats and any place easily reachable from a seated position.
- [S6.A] Controlled dangerous substances (illegal drugs, some prescription medications, chemical inhalants) can affect alertness, concentration, coordination, reaction time, distance judgment, and response to signals/sounds; the license can be suspended or revoked for driving on them; driving after taking a medication whose label warns against driving, when it impairs safe driving, can be a criminal offense.
- [S6.A] Planning fact stated in the manual: plan not to drive if you plan to drink; use a designated sober driver.
- [S6.B] Aggressive driving examples: speeding, tailgating, ignoring signals/devices, unsafe lane changes, not yielding, unsafe/illegal passing; triggers include crowded roads, delays, rushing, construction, stress; countermeasures include allowing extra time, patience, courtesy, signaling intentions, obeying laws/signs/signals/markings, yielding, not competing with other drivers.
- [S6.C] Distracted driving = taking eyes, hands, or mind off driving; stated as causing more police-reported crashes than anything else; examples: eating/drinking, adjusting radio/music, programming a GPS, dealing with children/pets, loose objects, talking/texting on a cell phone, smoking, makeup, shaving, reading, interacting with passengers.
- [S6.C] Handheld device ban: using a hand-held cell phone, including texting, while driving is illegal; hands-free calling is allowed for adults but should be minimal; safest practices stated: use the phone only in emergencies (passenger makes the call if possible), pull safely off the road and stop to call, let voicemail take incoming calls, keep calls short.
- [S6.C] Drivers under 18 may not use a cell phone while driving at all, even hands-free; the only exception is a 911 emergency call.
- [S6.D] Drowsy driving: sleepiness slows reaction time, decreases awareness, and impairs judgment like drugs/alcohol; higher-risk groups: people who drive many miles daily, people with sleep disorders, people on certain medications; danger signs: eyes closing/going out of focus, trouble keeping head up, repeated yawning, wandering thoughts, not remembering the last few miles, missing an exit, drifting out of lane, repeated speed changes; correct action: rest or change drivers.

## Section 7: Sharing the Road
- [S7.A] Pedestrians have the right-of-way at street crossings but must obey traffic control signals; with no signal, drivers must stop for pedestrians in a marked or unmarked crosswalk; most intersections have a crosswalk even if unmarked.
- [S7.A] Drivers must be especially alert for pedestrians who are deaf, blind, or mobility impaired (may need extra crossing time).
- [S7.A] A driver may not pass any vehicle stopped at a crosswalk; drivers must yield to pedestrians when turning on a green signal, and when turning on red after stopping where permitted.
- [S7.B] Emergency vehicles (police, ambulance, fire) using sirens/flashing lights have the right-of-way; on seeing/hearing one approach: move as close as possible to the edge of the road, stay out of intersections, remain stopped until it passes.
- [S7.B] A driver traveling in the same direction as an emergency vehicle using its signals may not pass it unless it stops or a police officer directs the driver to pass.
- [S7.B] Approaching a stopped emergency vehicle, tow truck, or other service vehicle: move to a lane not immediately adjacent to it; if that is not possible, slow to a safe speed and be ready to stop.
- [S7.C] Large trucks make wide turns and need more space; a truck turning right often moves left first; passing a truck on the right is risky; never cut in beside a truck as it begins a turn (risk of being trapped between truck and curb); if you stop past a stop line you may block trucks turning from a cross street.
- [S7.C] Passing a truck: pass quickly; return only when both of the truck's headlights are visible in your rear-view mirror; never cut in front of a truck; fully loaded trucks can weigh over 80,000 pounds and can take the length of a football field to stop; most cars weigh about 2,000 pounds.
- [S7.C] Truck/bus no-zones = four large blind spots (side, rear, front, backing); right-side blind spot runs the trailer's length and extends out three lanes; if you cannot see the driver's face in the side mirror, the driver cannot see you; trucks/buses need almost twice the time and room of cars to stop; never cross behind a backing truck (trucks have no rear-view mirror for this).
- [S7.D] School buses: when a school bus is stopped with flashing red lights, stop at least 20 feet behind it (if behind) or at least 20 feet in front of it (if approaching from the front); do not move again until the bus moves or turns off its flashing red lights; exception: on a multi-lane road divided by a physical barrier, traffic on the other side of the barrier does not stop.
- [S7.E] Motorcyclists have the same rights/privileges as other drivers; a motorcyclist is six times more likely than a car driver to be hurt in a crash; yield to an oncoming motorcycle when turning left (failure can bring a citation with significant penalties and cause serious injury; car drivers are at fault in over half of car-motorcycle crashes); look twice before lane changes/merges (mirrors + shoulder check); never share a lane with a motorcycle (riders may move within the lane for hazards, wind, visibility, and may use the whole lane); leave at least a 3 to 4-second following distance behind a motorcycle; motorcycles may slow without brake lights (e.g., for hazards); when passing, leave plenty of space (wind gusts/drafts affect motorcycles) and see the motorcycle's headlight in the rear-view mirror before returning; if a motorcycle passes you, maintain speed; for groups, signal early, wait for room, merge only between groups/riders with enough space, or slow and let a small group pass.
- [S7.F] Bicycles are vehicles under Maryland law; bicyclists may use the road and must obey all traffic signals; expect bicyclists on all road types except interstate highways and tolled bridges/tunnels, at intersections and traffic circles, in all weather, at all times.
- [S7.F] A bicyclist may use the full lane, even well below traffic speed, when the lane is too narrow for a motor vehicle to pass safely within it; bicyclists may ride in the center of the travel lane when the road is narrow, to avoid obstacles/hazards, on roads without shoulders, or where cars are parked along the right side.
- [S7.F] Bicyclists have the right-of-way when a motor vehicle is turning (drivers must yield); drivers entering or crossing a bike lane or shoulder must yield to bicyclists there; bicycles have the same right-of-way as pedestrians in a crosswalk; failing to yield to a bicycle resulting in a crash carries fines and/or prison time and points.
- [S7.F] Approaching a bicyclist: slow down; do not honk (bicyclists can usually hear vehicles; loud noise can startle them); do not follow too closely (bicycles can stop/swerve quickly for small hazards); check for bicyclists from behind before opening a car door.
- [S7.F] Passing a bicycle: leave at least 3 feet between your vehicle and the bicycle — in a bike lane, on the shoulder, or in the same lane; wait until oncoming traffic is clear; see the bicycle in the rear-view mirror before returning to your lane.
- [S7.F] At intersections: assume a bicyclist goes straight unless signaling a turn; yield to bicycles as to any vehicle; look both ways before crossing a sidewalk or trail; never turn right from a through lane immediately after passing a bike on a shoulder/bike lane; check the right side and right blind spot before a right turn; before a right turn, move as far right as possible within the bike lane/shoulder/right-turn lane; do not turn left immediately in front of a bicycle; watch for a bicyclist merging left when the road narrows (bridge, parked cars, obstructions).
- [S7.F] Bicycles are allowed on sidewalks statewide (local jurisdictions may prohibit); on sidewalks, bicyclists yield to pedestrians and people using assisted mobility devices.
- [S7.F] Riding at night: a dim reflective object may be a bicycle in a travel lane; approaching a bicycle at night, use low-beam headlights.
- [S7.F] Children on bicycles can be unpredictable, may change direction without warning, and may enter the road from driveways or near parked cars; obey school-zone and residential speed limits.
- [S7.G] Mopeds and motor scooters: riders must follow all traffic laws; they may be ridden on any road with a posted maximum speed limit of 50 mph or less; they may ride side-by-side or alone, on the road or on the shoulder; they should ride as close to the right side as safely possible.
- [S7.H] ADAS (lane keeping, blind spot warning, automatic emergency braking, etc.) assists but does not operate the vehicle; the driver stays responsible at all times; the technology has limitations (rain, snow, fog, hills, curves) and varies by vehicle; keep sensors clean and undamaged and software up to date.

## Section 8: Crashes and Traffic Stops
- [S8.A] Any crash: turn on emergency flashers or use flares to warn other drivers.
- [S8.A] Injury crash (including a pedestrian/bicyclist): remain at the scene; call 911 immediately (report number of people, injury type, location); do not move the vehicles.
- [S8.A] Leaving an injury crash: license revoked; up to 5 years in prison and up to a $5,000 fine; leaving a fatal crash is a felony: up to 10 years in prison and up to a $10,000 fine.
- [S8.A] No-injury crash, vehicle cannot move: call 911, give the location, report no injuries and that police assistance is needed; stay away from traffic; do not try to cross the road or stop traffic.
- [S8.A] No-injury crash, vehicle can move: move it off the road as close as possible to the crash location; exchange name, address, phone number, plate number/state, license number, vehicle make/model, insurance information; get witness names/addresses/phone numbers; record location, date/time, number of vehicles, weather, road conditions, damage, and how the crash happened; take pictures if possible.
- [S8.A] Police must always be called when: someone is injured; a vehicle cannot be moved; a driver appears under the influence of alcohol/drugs; a driver has no license; a driver leaves without giving insurance/other information; public property is damaged.
- [S8.A] Striking an unattended vehicle/property: stop as close as possible without blocking traffic more than necessary; try to find the owner and give your information; if the owner cannot be found, leave a visible note with what happened and your contact information.
- [S8.A] Hitting and injuring a domestic animal (dog, cat, horse, cow, etc.): call the police immediately.
- [S8.B] Disabled vehicle: move to the shoulder if possible; an off-ramp or parking lot is safer even if it means driving on a flat tire or damaging a rim; never stop/park in the triangular painted zones where a ramp exits; if staying in the vehicle, stay buckled and face forward; if leaving, pick a safe moment, move quickly away from the road, get behind an embankment/barrier/guardrail, move to higher ground if possible, face oncoming traffic.
- [S8.C] Traffic stop: pull off as far from traffic as possible; turn on flashers; turn off engine and radio; roll down the window; stay in the vehicle with seat belt fastened; keep hands in plain view (on the steering wheel is best); do not argue about a citation at the roadside (the case can be made in court).
- [S8.D] Move Over law: when approaching any vehicle stopped, standing, or parked on a road with its lights flashing, move over; if moving over is not safe/possible, slow to a safe speed considering weather, road conditions, traffic, and pedestrians.
- [S8.D] Move Over violation is a misdemeanor: $110 fine + 1 point; $150 fine + 3 points if the violation causes a crash; $750 fine + 3 points if there is a death or serious injury.
- [S8.D] The MVA uses points to track violations and issue penalties; points lead to suspension or revocation. (The manual states no general point-threshold table beyond the Move Over figures above.)

## Section 9: Other Restrictions, Violations and Penalties
- [S9.A] Driving is a privilege, not a right; the MVA may impose restrictions; driving in violation of a restriction is a serious offense that can cost driving privileges.
- [S9.A] Provisional License nighttime restriction: unsupervised driving between 12:00 midnight and 5:00 a.m. is allowed ONLY for: driving to/from/during a job; driving to/from an organized volunteer program; driving to/from an official school activity; driving to/from an athletic event or related training the holder participates in.
- [S9.A] Seat belt restriction (GLS holders under 18): the holder must always use a safety belt; all passengers must use safety belts or child safety seats.
- [S9.A] Passenger restriction: a Provisional License holder under 18 may not drive unsupervised with passengers under 18 for the first 151 days, unless they are immediate family members; a non-family passenger under 18 is allowed if a qualified supervising driver is present; immediate family = spouse, daughter, son, stepdaughter, stepson, sister, brother, stepsister, stepbrother, or a relative living at the holder's address.
- [S9.A] Wireless device restriction: Provisional License and Learner's Permit holders may not use any wireless communication device, including hands-free, while driving; the only exception is a 911 emergency call.
- [S9.B] Implied consent: driving or attempting to drive on a road or public-use private property in Maryland = consent to a drug/alcohol test if a law enforcement officer requests one.
- [S9.B] If an officer has reason to believe a driver is impaired by drugs, alcohol, or a controlled substance, a drug recognition expert may request a blood test.
- [S9.B] License will be suspended if BAC is 0.08 or higher; the MVA will also suspend the license if the driver is impaired with drugs in their system; if an officer submits a certified statement that the driver refused a test, the MVA will suspend the license.
- [S9.C] Trying to obtain a false/forged ID card, license, or learner's permit can bring fines and/or prison under federal and state law; lying about age to purchase, possess, or acquire alcohol is illegal and can also bring license suspension.
- [S9.D] The MVA may suspend, revoke, refuse, or cancel a license for motor vehicle law violations; the MVA mails a notice stating the planned action, the reason, and the steps to have it changed or stopped; in most cases an administrative hearing may be requested; a suspended/revoked/refused/canceled license must be surrendered to the MVA; suspension/revocation periods begin when the MVA receives the license.
- [S9.D] Suspension = temporary loss of driving privilege; the MVA may issue a restricted license for specific purposes (e.g., work, school).
- [S9.D] Revocation = more severe than suspension; no driving until the MVA determines the driver can drive safely again; a minimum waiting period applies before reinstatement can be requested; the MVA can deny the request.
- [S9.D] Cancellation = more severe than suspension or revocation; the privilege is terminated and not reinstated; grounds include: license should not have been issued, required/correct information not provided on the application, fraud in applying for or obtaining the license; a parent/legal guardian may request cancellation of a driver under 18.
- [S9.E] Provisional License moving-violation penalties (general): complete a driver improvement program and/or license suspension or revocation; after a suspension/revocation ends, an employment-and-education-only restriction is placed on the license.
- [S9.E] Minors (under 18) with a Provisional License, per moving-violation conviction/PBJ: 1st — driver improvement program; 2nd — 30-day suspension, then a 90-day employment-and-education-only restriction; 3rd — 180-day suspension, young-driver improvement program attendance, and a 180-day employment-and-education-only restriction; 4th or more — license revoked, and all licensing tests must be passed to get it back.
- [S9.E] Adults (18+) with a Provisional License, per moving-violation conviction/PBJ: 1st — driver improvement program; 2nd — 30-day suspension; 3rd or more — up to 180-day suspension or revocation; these penalties are in addition to court penalties; after each conviction/PBJ, a new 18-month conviction-free period must begin before advancing.
- [S9.F] Disability parking violations (big fines possible) include: parking in a disability space without an MVA disability plate/placard with the qualifying person as driver or passenger; blocking any part of a curb cut (even with a plate/placard); parking on any part of an access aisle next to a disability space (even with a plate/placard); using an expired placard; using a plate/placard without the person with the disability present and without proper identification; fraud/misrepresentation in applying for a plate/placard.
- [S9 note] A driver caught drinking and driving must install an ignition interlock (breathalyzer car lock) that prevents starting the vehicle if alcohol is detected.

## Section 10: Other Important Information
- [S10.A] Reportable medical conditions include: diabetes with a low-blood-sugar episode requiring another person's help in the past 6 months; epilepsy/seizures; a heart condition causing loss of consciousness in the past 6 months; stroke; dizzy spells/fainting/blackouts; sleep apnea or narcolepsy; history of traumatic brain injury; weakness/shaking/numbness in arms, hands, legs, or feet affecting driving; a missing/amputated/non-functioning hand, arm, foot, or leg affecting driving; an eye problem preventing corrected acuity of 20/70 in at least one eye or a binocular field of at least 110 degrees; alcohol use problem; drug use problem; a mental health condition that may affect driving; schizophrenia; dementia. Conditions must be reported at application/renewal, or when diagnosed after licensing; the Driver Wellness and Safety Division and/or Medical Advisory Board must approve licensing.
- [S10.B] Supervising driver: at least 21 years old; licensed for at least 3 years in Maryland or another state for the same vehicle class; seated beside the learner (unless the vehicle is a motorcycle); a learner may drive only the vehicle/class specified on the permit and only with a supervising driver.
- [S10.C] Organ donor: anyone 18+ may be a donor; minors at least 16 may be donors with written parental/guardian consent.
- [S10.D] Motor Voter: the National Voter Registration Act of 1993 requires the MVA to let eligible customers register to vote or update voter information when getting a license or ID card.
- [S10.E] Insurance: all Maryland-registered vehicles must be insured by a company licensed in Maryland, for personal injury and property damage liability in amounts required by law; the driver must always carry a valid insurance identification card (electronic format allowed) and show it to an officer on request (refusal can bring a fine); driving without insurance can bring a fine of up to $2,500 per vehicle per year.
- [S10.F] Seat belt law: the driver and all passengers must wear a safety belt or be buckled in a child safety seat.
- [S10.G] Child safety seats: all children under age 8 must ride in a child safety seat unless the child is 4 feet 9 inches or taller; children under age 2 must ride in a rear-facing seat meeting federal guidelines until reaching the manufacturer's height/weight limit; follow the seat and vehicle manufacturers' instructions; Kids In Safety Seats (KISS) can be reached at 800-370-SEAT for low-cost rental programs.
- [S10.H] Air bags: use with the lap and shoulder belt; sit as far back from the steering wheel as possible (try for 10–12 inches between steering wheel and chest); pregnant drivers place the lap belt under the abdomen, low on the hips across the upper thighs, and the shoulder belt over the rounding of the belly; a child in a rear-facing seat must never ride in the front seat of a vehicle with a passenger air bag; children under 13 should ride buckled in a rear seat.
- [S10.I] ABS: prevents wheel lock-up so steering control is kept; the system pumps the brakes faster than a driver can; engagement may cause brake noise (rumble) and pedal vibration.
- [S10.J] Headsets/earphones/earplugs in both ears are prohibited while driving; hearing aids are allowed.
- [S10.K] Parallel parking is no longer required on the driving test; the same skills are covered by the reverse two-point turn on the test course (visual skills, space judgment, mirrors and turn signals, steering, braking, acceleration control).
- [S10.L] Driving test maneuvers: pull-in parking, back-in parking, and a three-point turn must be performed successfully; parking is done in a 10 ft × 20 ft space; the turn in a 16 ft × 20 ft space.
- [S10.M] Bicycle equipment/rules (for bicyclists): helmets required for riders under 16, including passengers; brakes must stop the bicycle within 15 feet from 10 mph on dry, level, clean pavement; at night, a white front headlight visible from at least 500 feet and a red rear reflector visible from at least 600 feet; Maryland law also requires a front white headlight and rear red reflector visible from at least 600 feet when riding at night (visibility guidance in the same section); e-bike classes: Class 1 — pedal-assist, assistance stops at 20 mph; Class 2 — motor assists with or without pedaling, stops at 20 mph; Class 3 — pedal-assist, stops at 28 mph; e-bikes have operable pedals and a motor of 750 watts or less.
- [S10.N] Electric low-speed vehicles: designed to carry only the operator; weigh under 100 pounds; wheels in line (or one front, two back); handlebars and a standing platform; electric motor plus human power; maximum speed 20 mph; local restrictions may also apply.
